Buying a five- or six-figure watch from someone you have never met is an act of trust. Trust is exactly what scammers sell. They offer a rare reference at an attractive price, then steer you towards a payment method that gives you no way back.

**Escrow** is one of the best protections for this kind of deal, as long as it is genuine. This guide explains how escrow works for watches, what it does and does not protect, and how scammers fake it.

## Quick answer

In an escrow transaction, a neutral third party **holds the buyer's money** until the buyer has received the watch and confirmed it is as described. The seller ships once the money is secured, and the buyer has an **inspection period** to check the watch, ideally with an independent expert. If it is right, the escrow agent releases the money to the seller. If not, the deal is cancelled under the agreed terms. It only works if **you choose the escrow service yourself** and **go to its real website directly**. A seller who insists on "their" escrow is a red flag.

## How escrow works for a watch

1. **Agree the terms.** Buyer and seller settle on the exact watch (reference, serial number if possible, condition, included box and papers), the price and the inspection period.
2. **Open the transaction on an escrow service you have chosen.** Both parties create accounts and verify their identity.
3. **Buyer pays the escrow service,** not the seller.
4. **The escrow service confirms to the seller** that the funds are secured.
5. **Seller ships the watch,** with tracking and insurance, to the buyer (or sometimes to a third-party inspector first).
6. **Buyer receives the watch** and inspects it during the agreed period, ideally with an independent watchmaker or authenticator.
7. **Buyer accepts or rejects.** On acceptance, the escrow service pays the seller. On rejection, the watch goes back under the agreed rules, and the money is returned to the buyer, subject to the terms.

Some marketplaces offer escrow for high-value watches as part of the checkout, with a fixed inspection window, such as several days. Check the details of any programme before you rely on it.

## What escrow protects against

- **A seller who takes the money and ships nothing.** The seller does not get paid until the watch arrives and is accepted.
- **A buyer who receives the watch and refuses to pay.** The money is already with the escrow service.
- **A last-minute change of terms.** The agreed terms are recorded.

## What escrow does **not** do

| Limit | What it means for you |
| --- | --- |
| It does not authenticate the watch by itself | Escrow holds money. You still need to check authenticity, condition and parts |
| It cannot protect you from a fake escrow service | A scam site copies the real thing, so you must verify it |
| It does not cover shipping damage on its own | Insure the shipment, and agree who is responsible |
| It cannot fix poor terms | If the agreement is vague about condition, you may lose a dispute |
| It does not cover import duty and tax | Cross-border purchases can add costs |
| It adds time | Expect days or weeks for shipping and inspection |

## The fake escrow scam

Fake escrow is one of the most common frauds in high-value online sales, and government consumer-protection agencies have warned about it. A typical pattern:

1. A seller advertises a rare or discounted watch.
2. You agree to use escrow. The seller says, "I use a trusted service, let me send you the link," or suggests "their lawyer" or "their company's escrow."
3. The link goes to a polished website with a name or web address that looks like a real escrow service, sometimes with one letter changed.
4. You are told to **wire the money** to the "escrow" account.
5. The money is gone. The seller, and the "escrow service," disappear. In other versions, you receive a counterfeit or a watch with replaced parts.

Consumer-protection authorities note that scam artists sometimes use the names of well-known companies, create their own fake services, and give fake phone numbers so that you never speak to a real company.

### How to avoid it

- **You choose the escrow service,** not the seller. Reject "their preferred" service.
- **Type the web address yourself** or use a trusted bookmark. Do not follow links from emails and chats.
- **Check the address letter by letter.** Look for swapped, added or missing characters.
- **Look up the company's contact details** on its real website, not in the messages you were sent. Call a number you found independently.
- **Be wary of wire instructions** sent by email or chat, or of a request to pay by wire to an individual. A legitimate escrow service gives instructions inside your own account on its secure website.
- **Ask whether the escrow company is licensed** in the places where it operates, and check this with the regulator.
- **Walk away from pressure,** such as "the price is only good today."

## Payment methods compared

| Method | Protection if something goes wrong | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bank wire to the seller | Very little. Recovery is hard once funds are sent | Common in scams. Avoid for a stranger |
| Cryptocurrency | Very little. Transfers cannot easily be reversed | Avoid for a stranger |
| "Friends and family" style payments | Usually none | Often requested by scammers because they bypass buyer protection |
| Credit card through a regulated dealer | Possible chargeback rights, within limits and time windows | Check your card issuer's rules for large purchases and cross-border sales |
| Payment-platform buyer protection | Varies, often with limits and time windows | Read the terms for high-value goods |
| Independent escrow, used correctly | Strong, for the money, during the inspection period | Verify the service and the terms |
| Buying from an established dealer with a return policy and warranty | Depends on the dealer's terms | Often the simplest protection for a first high-value purchase |

## Before you pay: protecting the purchase itself

Escrow protects money, not authenticity. Combine it with checks on the watch:

- **Verify the seller.** Look for a long track record, real reviews and a physical business. Be cautious with new accounts and prices far below the market.
- **Ask for detailed photos and video,** including the serial number, the movement (if it is allowed to be shown), the clasp and the papers.
- **Check the paperwork.** Make sure the serial number and reference match the card or certificate, and that the seller's story about the watch's history makes sense.
- **Arrange an independent inspection.** An independent watchmaker or authenticator can examine the watch during the escrow inspection period. Agree the cost and who pays.
- **Agree the return rules in writing** if the watch is not as described, including condition, parts and any service history.
- **Insure the shipment,** and use tracked, signed-for delivery.
- **Check customs and tax.** For cross-border purchases, ask who handles import duty and tax, and how it affects the price.

## Fees and who pays

Escrow services charge fees, usually as a share of the transaction with a minimum, and the amount depends on the service, the value and the payment method. Buyer and seller can agree to split it, or to have one side pay. Always read the fee schedule before you start, and agree in writing who pays what. Cheaper is not always better. Licensing, support and dispute procedures matter more on a large purchase.

## Escrow checklist for a watch purchase

- ✅ You chose the escrow service yourself, not the seller
- ✅ You went to its website by typing the address or using a trusted bookmark
- ✅ The company is licensed where required, and its contact details match its real site
- ✅ Payment instructions appear inside your own secure account, not in an email or chat
- ✅ The agreement lists the exact reference, serial number, condition, papers and box
- ✅ The inspection period is long enough to get an independent check
- ✅ An independent watchmaker or authenticator is lined up
- ✅ Shipping is tracked, signed-for and insured, and responsibility for damage is agreed
- ✅ Return and rejection terms are written, including who pays shipping
- ✅ Customs duties and taxes are understood
- ✅ Fees are agreed in writing

## Red flags

- The seller insists on a specific escrow service, lawyer or "company escrow"
- A web address that is almost, but not exactly, a known escrow service
- Instructions to wire money to a person, or to a different name from the escrow company
- A price far below the market for a rare reference
- Pressure to move off the platform or to hurry
- The seller will not allow independent inspection
- A new account with few or unverifiable reviews
- A request to ship before the money is secured

## Common mistakes

- **Trusting a link sent by the seller.**
- **Assuming escrow means the watch is authentic.**
- **Skipping the independent inspection** because the inspection window is short.
- **Not insuring the shipment.**
- **Vague agreements** on condition and parts.
- **Paying by wire "just this once"** because the seller offered a discount.
- **Ignoring import duty and tax** on cross-border deals.

## FAQ

**Is escrow worth the fee on a watch?**
For a high-value purchase from someone you do not know, many buyers think so, because the fee is small compared with the risk. For a purchase from an established dealer with a return policy, it may be unnecessary.

**Can a seller use escrow too?**
Yes. Escrow also protects sellers, since the buyer's money is secured before the watch is shipped, which helps against fraudulent chargebacks and non-payment.

**Is escrow safe for a first-time buyer?**
It can be, if you choose a genuine service yourself and check it. Most losses come from fake escrow sites or from skipping authenticity checks.

**What if the watch is not as described?**
Follow the rejection process in your escrow agreement during the inspection period. Keep photos, documents and messages, and report fraud to the escrow service and the authorities in your country.

**Can I rely on this guide to authenticate a watch?**
No. This guide is about payment safety. Authenticity needs an independent expert, or the manufacturer or an authorised dealer.
