If you text customers in the UK, two sets of rules apply: **UK GDPR** (because a phone number is personal data) and the **Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 (PECR)**, which specifically govern electronic marketing. PECR is the one that most often catches senders out, and since 2025 it carries much heavier penalties.

This guide explains the rules in plain language, shows how they differ from the US and India, and gives you a checklist.

*This is general information, not legal advice. Rules change and the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) updates its guidance. Take advice from a UK lawyer for your own programme.*

## Quick answer

A marketing text counts as **electronic mail** under PECR. To text **individuals** (consumers, sole traders and some partnerships), you need **consent**, unless you can rely on the narrow **soft opt-in** for existing customers. Every marketing text must **identify your business** and give a **working way to opt out**. Texting **limited companies** does not need PECR consent, but the identity and opt-out rules still apply. Fines for PECR breaches can now reach **£17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover**, whichever is higher.

## What counts as marketing?

The ICO's approach is that if a message promotes products, services, aims or ideals, it is generally direct marketing, and the consent rules apply. That includes a message that mixes service information with a promotion.

A **service message** is different. It is a message that is necessary to deliver a service the person asked for, such as a delivery update, a booking confirmation or a one-time passcode. Service messages do not need marketing consent, but they must stay genuinely about the service. Adding an offer to the end can turn the whole message into marketing.

## Who is protected: individuals and corporate subscribers

| Recipient | Consent needed for marketing texts under PECR? |
| --- | --- |
| Individual subscribers: consumers, sole traders, certain partnerships | Yes, unless the soft opt-in applies |
| Corporate subscribers: limited companies, LLPs, public bodies | No PECR consent. Identity and opt-out rules still apply |

Be careful with business-to-business lists. A sole trader is an individual under PECR, even though they are a business customer, so you need their consent or a valid soft opt-in. UK GDPR can also apply when you process the personal data of a named person at a company, even if PECR consent is not required for that number.

## What valid consent looks like

Consent must be **freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous**, and given by a clear action. In practice:

- Say who will send the messages and **what kind of marketing** the person will receive, by text.
- Use a **separate, unticked** box or a clear opt-in action, not pre-ticked boxes or terms buried in small print.
- Do not bundle consent to marketing with consent to the service itself.
- Keep **records**: who agreed, when, how and to what wording.

## The soft opt-in

The soft opt-in lets you text **existing customers** without fresh consent, but only when all of these apply:

1. You obtained the person's contact details **directly from them** in the course of a **sale or negotiations for a sale**.
2. You are marketing **your own similar products or services**.
3. You gave them a **clear, simple chance to opt out** when you collected the details, and you offer an opt-out in **every message**.

Where it does not work:

- **Bought or shared lists.** The details must come from your own relationship with the person.
- **Group companies and partners.** Another organisation collecting sign-ups for you is not enough.
- **Marketing other businesses' products.**
- **People who only browsed or enquired** without a sale or negotiations for one, depending on the facts.

Treat the soft opt-in as a limited exception, not a general licence to message anyone who ever interacted with you.

The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 also extended a similar soft opt-in to **charities** for their charitable purposes. It does not change the rule for commercial senders.

## What every marketing text must show

Whoever receives it, a marketing text must:

- **Identify the sender**, and not disguise or conceal who you are.
- Give a **valid address or number** the recipient can use to **opt out**, free of charge apart from the cost of sending the message. A simple "Reply STOP to opt out" is common practice.

Respect opt-outs quickly, and add the number to a suppression list across every tool and vendor so that it cannot be messaged again from another system.

## Fines: what changed in 2025

The **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025** received Royal Assent on 19 June 2025. Among other changes, it **raised the maximum fines for PECR breaches** from £500,000 to **£17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover**, whichever is higher. This aligns PECR with the penalties under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. Different parts of the Act were commenced in phases by regulations, so check the current commencement position on GOV.UK.

Other points worth knowing:

- The ICO has a record of fining businesses for unsolicited marketing texts, and it uses complaints and spam reports as leads.
- PECR can catch both the business that sends a message and the business that **instigates** it. If you commissioned the campaign, you can be responsible, even if a provider pressed "send".
- The ICO can also issue warnings and reprimands.

## UK, US and India compared

| Topic | UK | US | India |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main rules | PECR and UK GDPR | TCPA, plus carrier rules and state laws | TRAI's TCCCPR and DLT, plus the DPDP Act |
| Consent for marketing texts | Needed for individuals, unless soft opt-in | Prior express written consent | Registered consent for promotional messages |
| Exception for existing customers | Soft opt-in, with conditions | No general exception for marketing | Limited, with rules on inquiry-based messages |
| Opt-out | Required in every marketing message | Required, with FCC rules on revocation | Required, with registered opt-out |
| Registration of senders or templates | No central registry for UK SMS | 10DLC brand and campaign registration | DLT entity, header and template registration |
| Maximum penalties | Up to £17.5 million or 4% of global turnover | Statutory damages per message, plus class actions | Provider action, blocking and disconnection |

## If you text UK customers from outside the UK

Being based in India or elsewhere does not take you outside UK rules for messages sent to people in the UK. Use a provider that supports UK sending, keep UK-style consent records, and check whether you need to transfer UK personal data lawfully to your own systems and vendors overseas. ## UK SMS marketing checklist

- ✅ You know whether each message is marketing or a genuine service message
- ✅ For individual subscribers, you have recorded consent or a valid soft opt-in
- ✅ Consent is specific to text marketing, unbundled and not pre-ticked
- ✅ The soft opt-in is used only for your own similar products to people who bought from you or negotiated to
- ✅ Every marketing text identifies your business
- ✅ Every marketing text includes a simple, free opt-out
- ✅ Opt-outs are processed quickly and synced across all tools and vendors
- ✅ Bought or shared lists are not used without proper consent
- ✅ You keep records of consent, soft opt-in eligibility and opt-outs
- ✅ A UK lawyer has reviewed your programme

## Common mistakes

- **Treating a sole trader as a company.** They are an individual under PECR.
- **Using the soft opt-in for enquiries** or for people who never bought.
- **Mixing offers into service messages.**
- **Buying lists** and relying on the seller's promise of consent.
- **Hiding the opt-out** or making it hard to use.
- **Forgetting that you can be responsible** for what your agency or provider sends for you.
- **Assuming the old £500,000 cap still applies.**

## FAQ

**Do I need consent to text a limited company's number?**
PECR does not require consent for corporate subscribers, but you must still identify yourself and give an opt-out. UK GDPR may still apply if the number is linked to a named person.

**Can I text people who bought from me last year?**
Possibly, under the soft opt-in, if you collected their details directly during the sale, you are marketing your own similar products, and you gave an opt-out at the time and in each message. Keep records that show this.

**Is "Reply STOP" enough as an opt-out?**
A simple reply keyword is widely used and fits the requirement for an easy, free opt-out. Make sure it works and is honoured promptly.

**Does the soft opt-in apply to charities?**
The 2025 Act extended a charitable-purpose soft opt-in with its own conditions. Charities should check the ICO's guidance.

**Are one-time passcodes marketing?**
No, a message needed to deliver a service the person requested is generally a service message. Keep it free of promotional content.
