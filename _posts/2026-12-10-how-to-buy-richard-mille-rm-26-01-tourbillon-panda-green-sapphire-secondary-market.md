## 1. The Multi-Million Dollar 1-of-1 High Horology Masterpiece

The **Richard Mille RM 26-01 Tourbillon Panda** in **Green Sapphire Crystal** is one of the most exclusive, high-concept artisanal timepieces ever created. Combining haute horlogerie, high-jewelry gem-setting, and micro-sculptural art, the dial features a hand-sculpted 18k white gold Giant Panda set with round-cut diamonds and black sapphires, munching on bamboo shoots crafted from hand-painted 18k yellow gold. Enclosed in an ultra-rare green sapphire crystal case, this reference stands as a true *Piece Unique* (1-of-1).

Because single-piece ultra-luxury assets command multi-million dollar valuations on the private secondary market, acquiring an RM 26-01 requires rigorous provenance verification to avoid synthetic green mineral cases, aftermarket gem-setting, or replica movement assemblies.

## 2. Synthetic Mineral Cases and Aftermarket Sculptures
* **What is the problem?** Counterfeiting syndicates attempt to replicate sapphire crystal cases using tinted glass or synthetic resin, while fitting third-party cast metal panda figurines over low-tier movements.
* **Why does it happen?** The extreme price delta between a standard luxury watch and a 1-of-1 high-jewelry Richard Mille tourbillon creates massive financial incentive for fraudulent secondary dealers to misrepresent custom aftermarket creations as factory original pieces.

## Due-Diligence Questions for the Green Piece Unique

- **What case colour and reference are recorded?** Ask the seller to show the certificate wording for the green sapphire case and the matching movement serial.
- **Can factory records be checked?** Request confirmation through an authorised boutique or archive route before committing funds.
- **Who created the panda and bamboo details?** Ask for documentation of the gem-setting and any later repairs to stones or paintwork.
- **When was the tourbillon last serviced?** Request workshop records and confirm the cage is functional.
- **Which presentation items are included?** Ask for a photographed list of the leather box, certificate, manual, straps, and fittings.

## Inspection Checklist in Escrow

- [ ] Examine the green case under UV and neutral light, asking a specialist to rule out resin tinting, bubbles, and colour streaks.
- [ ] Have a gemologist inspect the white diamonds and black sapphires for consistent setting and no adhesive bleed.
- [ ] Check the hand-engraved bamboo and panda surfaces for paint loss or later restoration.
- [ ] Observe the manual-wind tourbillon in motion and confirm the balance and cage behaviour.
- [ ] Inspect spline screws and case seals for tool marks or unauthorised opening.
- [ ] Photograph certificate, movement serial, and the complete set before payment.

## Common Mistakes and FAQ

**Does a green tint confirm grown sapphire?** No. Ask for non-destructive material testing rather than relying on colour.

**Do diamonds and a panda figure prove factory origin?** No. Gem-setting quality and provenance are separate checks; both should be documented.

**How should I treat resale expectations?** As a one-of-one, it should be judged on its own merits rather than on assumed parity with the blue variant.
