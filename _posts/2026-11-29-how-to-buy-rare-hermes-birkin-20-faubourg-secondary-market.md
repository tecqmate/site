## 2. The Problem: Navigating Extreme Rarity & Multi-Leather Fakes
* **What is the problem?** Grail editions—such as the **Hermès Neige Snow Faubourg 20** or the **Faubourg Minuit Midnight**—are frequently targeted by counterfeit syndicates who attempt to fuse authentic Hermès hardware onto altered leather bodies.
* **Why does it happen?** Retail prices for standard quota bags like a **Birkin 25 Togo** sit around $13,500, while limited edition Faubourgs clear $150,000 to $400,000 at auction. The massive value gap incentivizes deceptive secondary listings.

## 3. What to Do: The Ultra-Luxury Faubourg Inspection Matrix

Before wiring funds for high-value limited-edition bags like a **Birkin 20 Faubourg**, **Birkin Cargo 25**, or **Birkin Picnic 25**, insist on verifying these non-negotiable physical markers:

1. **Audit Multi-Leather Stitching Transitions:** The Faubourg features five separate leather cuts joined in tight architectural proximity. Inspect the seam transitions between *Matte Alligator* (awning) and *Epsom/Swift* (walls) under a 10x jeweler's loupe. Authentic Hermès saddle stitching uses beeswax-coated linen thread slanted at a uniform angle with zero loose thread ends.
2. **Examine Architectural Clochette Detail:** The leather clochette on a genuine Faubourg 20 is custom-designed to mimic the iconic Hermès orange shopping bag. Counterfeits often ship with standard-issue Birkin key clochettes rather than the limited-edition mini shopping bag charm.
3. **Inspect Blind Stamps & Year Codes:** Verify that the date stamp location (inside the left interior wall for modern bags) matches official production years. First-edition Day and Night Faubourgs were released in 2019 (D Stamp), while Midnight and Snow editions carry later Y/Z stamps.
