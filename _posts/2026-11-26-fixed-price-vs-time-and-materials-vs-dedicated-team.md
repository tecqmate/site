Before you compare hourly rates or total quotes, decide **how** you will pay. The contract model changes who carries the risk, how flexible the project can be, and how much you will really spend.

Most software vendors offer three models: **fixed price**, **time and materials**, and a **dedicated team**. Each fits a different kind of project. Picking the wrong one is a common reason for budget disputes.

## Quick answer

- **Fixed price** suits small, well-defined projects where the scope will not change.
- **Time and materials (T&M)** suits projects where requirements will evolve, and you want flexibility and visibility.
- **Dedicated team** suits long-term product work where you need steady capacity, like an extension of your own team.

If your scope is unclear, do not force it into a fixed price. Either clarify the scope first with a short discovery phase, or choose a flexible model with clear limits.

## Model 1: Fixed price

You and the vendor agree on a scope, a total price and a timeline. You pay according to milestones.

**Works well when:**
- The scope is detailed and stable (for example, a brochure website, a simple internal tool or a clearly defined integration)
- You have a fixed budget and need cost certainty
- You have written requirements, designs or a good project brief

**Strengths**
- Predictable total cost
- Less day-to-day involvement from you
- Easy to compare quotes

**Risks**
- Changes are handled through change requests, which can be slow and expensive
- Vendors may add a risk margin to the price
- The vendor has an incentive to do the minimum that meets the written scope
- If the scope was poorly defined, disputes about "what was included" are common

**Tip:** Define acceptance criteria for each milestone in writing. See our guide on [writing a software project brief]({{ '/2026/11/23/how-to-write-software-project-brief-rfp-template/' | relative_url }}).

## Model 2: Time and materials (T&M)

You pay for the actual time spent, usually at an hourly or daily rate per role, often billed monthly. The vendor gives an estimate, but it is not a guarantee.

**Works well when:**
- Requirements are uncertain or will change as you learn from users
- You are building a new product and expect to iterate
- You want to start quickly without a long specification phase

**Strengths**
- Flexibility to change priorities
- You pay only for the work done
- Good fit for agile delivery in short cycles

**Risks**
- The final cost is not fixed
- You need to manage priorities and review progress regularly
- Without controls, costs can grow quietly

**Controls to ask for**
- A monthly or sprint-level budget cap
- Weekly or fortnightly demos of working software
- Time reports that show who worked on what
- A clear process to approve extra work before it starts

## Model 3: Dedicated team

You hire a team (developers, QA, designer, project manager) from the vendor who work mainly on your product, usually for a minimum period such as several months. You pay a monthly fee per team member.

**Works well when:**
- You have an ongoing product roadmap, not a one-off project
- You need to scale capacity up or down
- You want continuity and growing product knowledge in the team

**Strengths**
- Stable team that learns your business
- Easier long-term planning
- You steer the priorities directly

**Risks**
- You pay for capacity even in quieter weeks
- You need a clear product owner on your side
- Quality depends on the people assigned, so ask who they are
- Moving to another vendor later can be hard if knowledge sits only in their team

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor | Fixed price | Time and materials | Dedicated team |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cost certainty | High | Low to medium | Medium (monthly) |
| Flexibility for changes | Low | High | High |
| Your involvement | Low | Medium to high | High |
| Best for | Clear, small scope | Evolving scope | Long-term product work |
| Main risk | Scope disputes | Cost drift | Paying for idle capacity |
| Typical start | Needs a detailed brief | Can start with a rough plan | Needs a roadmap and product owner |

## A hybrid that often works: discovery first

Many teams combine models:

1. **Paid discovery phase (fixed, short).** The vendor works with you to define scope, user flows, risks and an estimate.
2. **Fixed price for the defined core.** For example, version one.
3. **T&M or a small team afterwards.** For improvements and new features.

This gives you cost certainty where the work is clear, and flexibility where it is not.

## How to choose: five questions

1. **How clear is my scope?** If you can write it down in detail, fixed price is possible. If not, lean towards T&M or discovery first.
2. **How likely is it to change?** Frequent changes make fixed price painful.
3. **Is this a project or a product?** Projects end. Products keep growing, which favours a dedicated team.
4. **How much time can I spend managing?** T&M and dedicated teams need more of your attention.
5. **How firm is my budget?** If it is strict, fixed price or a capped T&M arrangement gives more control.

## Contract points to check, whichever model you choose

- **Ownership of code and designs** after payment
- **Access to repositories, servers and accounts** from day one, in your name
- **Acceptance criteria** and how bugs found after delivery are handled
- **Warranty or support period** after launch
- **Confidentiality** and data protection terms
- **Termination rights** and handover obligations
- **Who is on the team**, and what happens if a key person leaves
- **Payment schedule** tied to deliverables, not only to dates

Have a qualified lawyer review the final contract, especially for cross-border work.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing fixed price just because it feels safe.** A fixed price on a vague scope often leads to disputes or reduced quality.
- **Choosing T&M with no controls.** Flexible does not have to mean unlimited.
- **Comparing hourly rates only.** A cheaper rate with slower delivery can cost more overall.
- **Skipping discovery** on a complex project.
- **No product owner** on your side, whichever model you use.

## FAQ

**Which model is cheapest?**
None is always cheapest. Fixed price gives certainty but may include a risk margin. T&M can be cheaper if you manage scope well. A dedicated team is efficient for continuous work but not for short projects.

**Can I switch models mid-project?**
Yes, but agree the rules for switching in the contract. A common route is fixed price for version one, then T&M or a team afterwards.

**How do I check progress on a T&M project?**
Ask for regular demos, a shared task board, time reports and a monthly summary of budget used against plan.

**Is a dedicated team the same as hiring staff?**
No. The vendor employs the people and handles HR, equipment and management. You direct the work and pay a monthly fee.
