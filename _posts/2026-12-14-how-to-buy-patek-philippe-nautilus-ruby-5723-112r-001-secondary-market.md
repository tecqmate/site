## The Multi-Million Dollar Factory Gem-Set Grail

The **Patek Philippe Nautilus Reference 5723/112R-001** in 18k rose gold is one of the most exclusive factory gem-set sports timepieces in modern horology. Featuring a sunburst brown dial with horizontal embossed ridges, the watch is distinguished by its factory bezel channel-set with 32 flawless baguette-cut natural rubies (approx. 4 carats), complemented by baguette ruby hour markers at 6, 9, and 12 o'clock.


Reserved almost exclusively for Patek Philippe's most prominent global clients, secondary market acquisition is the only avenue for most collectors to secure this reference. However, trading at multi-million dollar valuations creates extreme incentive for illicit dealers to present aftermarket gem-set 5711 conversions as authentic factory 5723 pieces.

## Aftermarket Ruby Swaps and Misrepresented Base References
* **What is the problem?** Unscrupulous secondary dealers take standard rose gold 5711/1R models, cut the bezel, and install aftermarket rubies or synthetic stones to impersonate a factory 5723/112R-001.
* **Why does it happen?** The price delta between a standard rose gold Nautilus and a genuine factory gem-set Reference 5723 exceeds seven figures. Detecting custom gem settings requires forensic gemological auditing and certificate verification.

## A 4-Step Secondary Market Buying Protocol

If you are committing high-tier capital to acquire a Patek Philippe 5723/112R-001, follow this buyer-protection workflow:

* **Step 1: Require Guarantee Card Verification:** Ensure the original Patek Philippe Certificate of Origin explicitly lists the full reference code `5723/112R-001`. Never accept verbal claims of "factory settings" without matching paperwork.
* **Step 2: Mandate Independent Gemological & Horological Escrow:** Utilize specialized escrow vaults where an accredited gemologist verifies that all 32 bezel rubies and dial markers are natural, unheated gems set by Patek Philippe workshops.
* **Step 3: Audit Lug & Clasp Hallmarks Under Loupe:** Inspect the 18k 750 gold hallmarks, St. Bernard dog head, and Patek Philippe crown stamps on the caseback and fold-over clasp to verify deep, crisp laser outlines.
* **Step 4: Perform Serial & Archive Cross-Check:** Confirm that the movement and case serial numbers match official factory archives and boutique delivery records.

## A Model-Specific Brief: Why the 5723/112R-001 Needs a Forensic Check

The reference on the papers has to match the object in the case, and this model makes that easier to say than to verify. A factory 5723/112R-001 is an 18k rose gold Nautilus with a bezel set with baguette rubies and ruby hour markers at 6, 9 and 12, paired with a sunburst brown dial. A standard rose gold 5711/1R has a plain bezel; converting one means cutting a new seat for stones and fitting replacement markers. At a glance the result can look convincing, so the inspection should focus on how the setting was made rather than on the overall sparkle. Ask an independent specialist to examine the bezel profile, the channel walls and the relationship between the stone tables and the bezel surface under magnification. Compare the case and clasp engravings with the reference photographs you brought, and treat any sign of a reworked bezel as a reason to stop and investigate further.

## Gemstone and Setting Checklist

- Ask a qualified gemologist to confirm whether the rubies are natural and whether any treatments are present; do not accept a generic “genuine stones” statement on a sales invoice.
- Under 10x magnification, look for consistent stone sizes, even table heights and clean channel seats. Visible adhesive, mismatched saturation or loose stones warrant an independent opinion.
- Check the three baguette markers at 6, 9 and 12 for alignment with the printed minute track and consistent setting depth.
- Ask whether the watch has been polished or refinished; heavy polishing can soften hallmark edges and change how a bezel sits.
- Have the movement reference and serial checked against the paperwork and, where available, an official archive extract.

## Questions for the Seller

Ask the seller to state exactly how the reference is written on the Certificate of Origin and guarantee card, and to provide a photograph of the card that shows the full code. Ask when and where the watch was delivered, whether it has been serviced by an authorised centre, and whether any stones have been replaced. Request that the serial and movement numbers be supplied before you pay a deposit so that preliminary checks can be made. Confirm that the escrow arrangement permits a gemological and horological inspection before funds are released.

## Common Mistakes

The main mistake is reading a matching serial number as proof that the gem-setting is factory original. Another is judging rubies by colour alone under showroom lighting; lighting changes apparent saturation dramatically. Buyers also forget that a high price is not evidence — verify the object, not the number on the invoice. ## FAQ

**Can a lab report replace a manufacturer document?** No. A lab can speak to the stones; the Certificate of Origin and archive check speak to the watch. You need both lines of evidence.

**Is any colour variation in rubies suspicious?** Natural stones often vary slightly. Uniformity should be assessed by a specialist who knows what is typical for a factory-set piece.
