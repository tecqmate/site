If you have asked for website quotes, you have probably seen numbers that do not look like they belong to the same project: one at $600, another at $28,000. The difference usually comes down to **scope, who builds it, and what is included**. This guide explains typical price ranges in 2026 and what actually moves the price.

*Ranges below are compiled from several published 2026 pricing guides and are broad on purpose. Your quote depends on your exact needs. This is general information, not a quote.*

## Typical cost ranges by type of website

| Website type | Typical range (USD) | Typical timeline |
|---|---|---|
| DIY with a website builder | About $100 to $500 to start | Days to a few weeks |
| Brochure site (1 to 10 pages) | About $500 to $6,000 (freelancer or small agency) | 2 to 6 weeks |
| Business site with a CMS (10 to 15 pages) | About $3,000 to $15,000 | 4 to 10 weeks |
| Ecommerce store (Shopify or WooCommerce) | About $3,000 to $12,000 (freelancer), about $8,000 to $25,000 or more (agency) | 8 to 16 weeks |
| Custom web app or platform | About $25,000 to $150,000 or more | 3+ months |

**UK:** a four-to-six-page brochure site from a competent freelancer or small agency is commonly quoted at about £1,500 to £3,500.

**India:** small business sites are commonly quoted at about ₹5,000 to ₹50,000 for basic template-based work, and around ₹40,000 to ₹1,50,000 for professionally designed sites. Custom sites with a CMS and integrations run from about ₹1,50,000 to ₹6,00,000 or more, and ecommerce built by an agency is often ₹1,50,000 to ₹4,00,000 or more.

Agencies based in the US or UK usually charge more than equally capable teams in lower-cost regions. Quality does not always rise with price, so judge by portfolio, process, and references.

## Who builds it changes the price

| Option | Best for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| DIY builder | Very small budgets, simple sites | You do the design and content work, with limited flexibility |
| Freelancer | Small, well-defined projects | One person, so check availability and backup |
| Agency or studio | Business-critical or complex sites | Higher cost, more process and support |
| In-house developer | Ongoing, large product work | Salary and management overhead |

## The 8 biggest cost drivers

1. **Number of pages and complexity.** More page types and layouts mean more design and testing.
2. **Template or custom design.** A customised template costs far less than a fully custom design.
3. **Functionality.** Online payments, bookings, member areas, search, and multi-language support all add work.
4. **Integrations.** Connecting to a CRM, payment gateway, accounting tool, or inventory system adds time and testing.
5. **Content.** Copywriting, photography, video, and product data entry are often excluded from quotes and can add significantly.
6. **SEO, speed, and accessibility.** Proper technical SEO, performance tuning, and accessibility work cost extra but pay off.
7. **Security and compliance.** Cookie consent, privacy pages, GDPR and UK GDPR requirements, and secure hosting need setup.
8. **Timeline.** Rush delivery usually costs more because more people or overtime are needed.

## Costs that come after launch

A website is an ongoing asset, not a one-off purchase. Plan for:

* **Domain and hosting.** Billed yearly, and rising with traffic and features.
* **Maintenance and security updates.** Many guides suggest budgeting roughly 10% to 20% of the build cost per year, and sometimes more for busy ecommerce sites. Monthly plans commonly run from about $15 to $150 or more.
* **Content updates and improvements.** New pages, offers, and fixes.
* **Paid tools and plugins.** Licences for forms, security, SEO, and email.

One UK guide estimates the realistic total cost of ownership for a professionally built small business site at about £2,000 to £4,000 per year once hosting, maintenance, and improvements are included.

## Hidden costs to ask about

* Content creation, translation, and stock photos.
* Premium themes, plugins, and licences, and who pays renewals.
* Who owns the domain, hosting account, and source files.
* Training and handover after launch.
* Charges for revisions beyond the agreed rounds.

## How to compare quotes fairly

1. **Give every vendor the same written brief.**
2. **Ask what is included and what is not,** especially content, SEO, security, and training.
3. **Check the process:** discovery, design approval, testing, launch, and support.
4. **Ask for milestone-based payments** rather than most of the fee upfront.
5. **Confirm ownership.** You should own the site, content, and accounts.
6. **Check the support period** and what bug fixes are free.
7. **Verify past work and references.**

## Ways to lower cost without hurting quality

* **Start smaller.** Launch with the pages that earn business and add more later.
* **Use a proven platform** such as a mainstream CMS or ecommerce platform instead of building from scratch.
* **Provide your own content** and images, which often cuts cost noticeably.
* **Phase features.** Launch the essentials first, then add extras.
* **Consider a vendor in a lower-cost region,** with proper due diligence and a clear contract.

## Red flags in a quote

* A very low price with no scope or deliverables.
* No mention of testing, security, or support.
* Vendor keeps ownership of your domain, hosting, or code.
* Pressure to pay the full amount upfront.
* Vague answers about who will do the work.

## Quick budgeting checklist

* Define the site type and must-have features.
* Prepare content and images, or budget for them.
* Get at least three written quotes on the same brief.
* Add 10% to 20% per year for maintenance and hosting.
* Confirm you own the domain, accounts, and code.
* Plan a launch date with buffer for revisions.

## FAQ

**Is a cheap website bad?**
Not necessarily. A small, template-based site can be perfectly adequate. Problems come from unclear scope and hidden extras.

**How long does it take to build a business website?**
A simple brochure site often takes 2 to 6 weeks, a business site 4 to 10 weeks, and an ecommerce site 8 to 16 weeks, depending on how fast content and approvals arrive.

**Do I need to pay for maintenance?**
You need someone to apply updates, backups, and security fixes. This can be you, your vendor, or a maintenance plan.
