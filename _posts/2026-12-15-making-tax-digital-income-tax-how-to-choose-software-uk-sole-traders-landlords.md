If you are a UK sole trader or landlord, the annual Self Assessment routine is changing. **Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax** replaces the once-a-year scramble with digital records, quarterly updates to HMRC and a final digital return. To do any of it, you need software that is compatible with HMRC's system.

This guide explains who is affected, the key dates, what the software has to do, and how to choose one without overpaying or ending up with something that does not fit.

*This guide is general information, not tax advice. Check the current rules on GOV.UK and talk to an accountant about your own situation.*

## Quick answer

MTD for Income Tax has applied since **6 April 2026** to sole traders and landlords with qualifying income **over £50,000**. It extends to those over **£30,000** from **6 April 2027** and over **£20,000** from **6 April 2028**. HMRC does not provide the software, so you must choose compatible software (or an accountant who uses it). Pick it by how you keep your records today, whether you need a bank feed, who else uses it (your accountant) and what it will cost over a full year.

## Who has to use it, and when

| Tax year used to test your income | Qualifying income | You must start using MTD for Income Tax from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2024 to 2025 | More than £50,000 | 6 April 2026 |
| 2025 to 2026 | More than £30,000 | 6 April 2027 |
| 2026 to 2027 | More than £20,000 | 6 April 2028 |

"Qualifying income" means your gross income from self-employment and property combined, before expenses. Only sole traders and landlords are currently required to join. Limited companies are not covered by MTD for Income Tax, although you may already use MTD for VAT if you are VAT registered.

HMRC has said that around 1,077,000 more sole traders and landlords will be brought in from April 2027, so a lot of people are about to look for software at the same time.

## What changes in practice

Instead of one annual return, you will:

1. **Keep digital records** of your income and expenses, in compatible software.
2. **Send quarterly updates** to HMRC, every three months.
3. **Finish the year** with a final declaration, which includes your other income and allowances. It is due by 31 January after the tax year ends.

Quarterly updates do not replace the tax return. You still have to submit the final declaration and pay any tax due.

## Quarterly update deadlines

For the standard update periods, the deadlines are:

| Update period | Deadline |
| --- | --- |
| 6 April to 5 July | 7 August |
| 6 July to 5 October | 7 November |
| 6 October to 5 January | 7 February |
| 6 January to 5 April | 7 May |

If your accounting period runs from 1 April to 31 March, some software lets you choose **calendar update periods** (1 April to 30 June, and so on) to make record keeping simpler. The deadlines stay the same. Choose this option in your software before you send the first update for the tax year, because you cannot go back to standard periods until the next tax year once you have sent an update.

For people already in MTD, the next quarterly update after today is due on **7 February 2027**, followed by **7 May 2027**. Missing deadlines can lead to points-based late submission penalties, and reaching the threshold brings a £200 penalty. Check GOV.UK for the current details.

## What compatible software must be able to do

HMRC says your software, or your agent's, needs to let you:

- Create, store and correct digital records of your self-employment and property income and expenses
- Send your quarterly updates to HMRC
- Complete and submit your final declaration (tax return)

You can use one all-in-one product, or more than one product that work together. If you combine a spreadsheet with other software, the records need **digital links** between each step, so that figures are not retyped by hand. Digital record keeping does not mean you must scan and store every receipt digitally.

HMRC does not produce software. It publishes a list of compatible products on GOV.UK. Use that list to check any product you are considering.

## The main ways to set it up

| Setup | How it works | Often suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All-in-one accounting software | Records, bank feed, quarterly updates and final declaration in one place | Sole traders who want everything in one tool |
| Bookkeeping app that links to your bank | Transactions flow in from your bank, and you categorise them | Busy people with many small transactions |
| Spreadsheet plus bridging software | You keep records in a spreadsheet, and bridging software sends the figures to HMRC | People happy with spreadsheets, with digital links in place |
| Your accountant or agent does it | The agent uses their software and you supply records | People who prefer to hand it over |
| Landlord-focused tools | Built around rental income and property expenses | Landlords with several properties |

## How to choose: 10 questions

1. **Is it on HMRC's compatible software list?** Check this first.
2. **Does it cover everything you need?** That means digital records, quarterly updates and the final declaration, not just one of them.
3. **Does it cover all your income types?** Self-employment, property, or both.
4. **Can it connect to your bank?** A bank feed saves time, and many UK banks support one.
5. **How do you keep records today?** If you use spreadsheets, ask how digital links and bridging work.
6. **Will your accountant work with it?** Ask them which tools they support, or whether they can act as your agent.
7. **What does it cost over a full year?** Check the price per month, what changes at renewal and which features cost extra.
8. **Is it easy enough for you?** Use a free trial with your real data for a few weeks, not just the demo.
9. **What happens to your data if you leave?** Check that you can export your records.
10. **What support is available?** Think about the days around a deadline, when everyone needs help at once.

## If you are VAT registered

If you already use Making Tax Digital for VAT, check whether your VAT software also supports MTD for Income Tax. Using one product for both can save duplicate entry, but make sure each part meets HMRC's requirements.

## Red flags

- A product that is not on HMRC's compatible software list
- Pricing that is unclear, or that changes sharply at renewal
- No way to export your data
- Promises that you will "never need an accountant"
- No trial or no clear support route
- Pressure to buy a long contract before you have tried it

## What to do now, depending on your situation

**If you are already in MTD (income over £50,000):**
- Confirm you have submitted all quarterly updates so far, and check the next deadline, 7 February 2027.
- Check that your records are digital and kept up to date.

**If you will join in April 2027 (income over £30,000):**
- Use the next few months to try software, ideally on your real records.
- Talk to your accountant about the transition.
- Decide whether to keep a spreadsheet or move to an app.

**If you will join in April 2028 (income over £20,000):**
- You have more time, but starting good digital habits early makes the change easier.

## MTD software checklist

- ✅ Confirmed whether and when MTD for Income Tax applies to you, using your qualifying income
- ✅ Product is on HMRC's compatible software list
- ✅ It handles digital records, quarterly updates and the final declaration
- ✅ It covers both self-employment and property income, if you have both
- ✅ Bank feed and receipt options match how you work
- ✅ Your accountant is happy to use it, or has recommended it
- ✅ You know the full yearly cost, and what happens at renewal
- ✅ You have tried it with your own data before committing
- ✅ You can export your records if you change software
- ✅ The next quarterly deadline is in your calendar

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting until just before the first deadline** to choose software.
- **Assuming a spreadsheet is enough** without digital links or bridging software.
- **Choosing on price alone,** then discovering key features cost extra.
- **Not telling your accountant** and ending up with tools that do not work together.
- **Forgetting the final declaration.** Quarterly updates do not replace it.
- **Missing the choice of calendar periods** before the first update of the year.

## FAQ

**Does MTD for Income Tax apply to limited companies?**
No. It applies to sole traders and landlords. Limited companies are not required to use it.

**Does HMRC provide free software?**
HMRC does not provide MTD software. Some third-party products have free options, and HMRC lists compatible software on GOV.UK. Check what the free version includes.

**Can I still use a spreadsheet?**
Yes, if the records are digital and the steps from your spreadsheet to HMRC are linked digitally, usually through bridging software. Check HMRC's rules on digital links.

**What if my income is close to the threshold?**
HMRC works from your qualifying income as defined in its guidance, and you may be told by HMRC that you need to join. If you are near a threshold, ask your accountant.

**What if I need a custom tool?**
