India is a major producer of agarbatti (incense sticks), and buyers around the world source scented sticks, dhoop and related products from Indian makers. The trade is friendly to newcomers, but the paperwork and the raw materials have a few traps, especially where **agarwood** and other protected materials are involved.

We have written several detailed guides on this product. This page pulls them together into one **checklist for buyers**, with the key export rules in plain language.

*This guide is general information, not legal, tax or customs advice. Rates, policies and notifications change, so confirm the current position with a customs broker, a Chartered Accountant or the official DGFT and CBIC sources.*

## Quick answer

Agarbatti is classified under **HSN 3307 41 00** ("agarbatti and other odoriferous preparations which operate by burning"), and its export policy is listed as **free**. Check that your supplier has an **IEC**, GST registration and clean export paperwork, and that **no protected raw materials** (such as agarwood from the wild) are in the product without the right permits. Sort out your own country's import and labelling rules before you place an order, then follow the usual import steps: samples, supplier checks, inspection, Incoterms, safe payment and documents.

## What an Indian exporter needs

As a buyer, check that your supplier has:

1. **An Import Export Code (IEC).** Without it, they cannot export in their own name.
2. **A GST registration** that is active, with legal names matching the IEC.
3. **A way to handle GST on exports.** Exports are generally treated as zero-rated, either by exporting under a letter of undertaking (LUT) without paying IGST, or by paying IGST and claiming a refund. The supplier's accountant decides this, but you should know that your price quotation depends on it.
4. **Export paperwork.** A commercial invoice, packing list, shipping bill and transport documents, plus a certificate of origin if you need one.
5. **A real production base.** Ask whether they manufacture the sticks or trade them, and who makes your product.

## Raw materials: where the compliance risk sits

An agarbatti is made of a bamboo stick, a base of charcoal or wood powder, a binder, and fragrance and resins. Some of these inputs are regulated.

### Agarwood (oud)

Agarwood comes from *Aquilaria* trees, which are listed in **Appendix II of CITES**. India restricts the export of agarwood derivatives because of this listing, and exports need a **CITES export permit** and other conditions. The government has set export quotas for cultivated agarwood, and has said that wild-sourced agarwood has a zero export quota.

A DGFT notification of 30 October 2026 (No. 46/2026) amended the export licensing notes so that agarwood oil, chips and allied products can be exported through the Guwahati airport, subject to the requirements. The CITES permit requirement stays.

**What buyers should do:** if a product claims to contain agarwood or oud, ask for the supplier's documents showing the material's origin and the permits for export. Be cautious about "pure oud" products offered at very low prices, which may not contain agarwood at all, or may contain illegally sourced material.

### Damar and other resins

Damar batu and similar resins are widely used. Our guide on [Damar Batu grades and buying]({{ '/2026/11/17/damar-batu-grades-uses-buying-guide-incense-makers/' | relative_url }}) explains grades and how to buy.

### Other natural materials

Sandalwood, some animal-derived materials and certain wood species can have their own export rules. If a product's selling point is a rare natural ingredient, ask for written proof that it can be exported legally, and check your own country's import rules for it.

## Rules in your own country

The exporter handles Indian rules. **Your** country's rules are your responsibility as importer. Before you order, check with a customs broker:

- Duty and tax rates for your HS code, including any trade-agreement benefits
- Product safety, labelling and language requirements
- Rules on fragrance ingredients and chemicals that apply to burnable products
- Rules for plant-based and wood-based materials, including packaging
- Whether an importer registration is needed

Requirements differ by country. For the US, see our guide on [importing from India to the US]({{ '/2026/11/19/importing-from-india-to-the-us-step-by-step-guide/' | relative_url }}).

## If you are an Indian maker planning to export

Buyers look for a few things. Make sure you have your IEC and GST in order, a consistent product with samples that match production, clear specifications and packaging, and the right documents. If you are still starting out, our guide to the [agarbatti making business, training and government support]({{ '/2026/11/17/agarbatti-making-business-india-ffdc-training-government-support/' | relative_url }}) covers courses and support schemes.

## Quality and packaging points specific to incense

- **Burn quality:** even burn, steady scent, low smoke for the type, and clean ash.
- **Consistency:** the same scent and strength across batches.
- **Moisture:** sticks absorb damp, so packaging and storage matter on long sea journeys.
- **Scent:** strong smells can transfer between cartons. Pack and load carefully.
- **Breakage:** check stick strength, carton size and cushioning.
- **Labelling:** include the product name, net quantity, burn time if you claim one, safety instructions, country of origin and your own market's required information.

## Red flags

- A supplier with no IEC, or with names that differ across records
- "Pure oud" or agarwood claims at prices far below the market
- No answer about where the raw materials come from
- Pressure to pay in full in advance
- A sample that is much better than a production batch
- No willingness to allow an independent inspection

## Agarbatti export buyer checklist

- ✅ Product specification written, including type, fragrance, pack and quantity
- ✅ HS and HSN codes confirmed, and duties and taxes checked for your country
- ✅ Your own country's import and labelling rules checked with a customs broker
- ✅ Supplier's IEC, GST and company records verified, and production capacity confirmed
- ✅ No agarwood or protected material, or the right permits are in place
- ✅ Several sample batches tested before the order
- ✅ Incoterm and named place agreed in writing
- ✅ Payment terms tied to inspection or documents
- ✅ Independent pre-shipment inspection booked
- ✅ Documents checked against each other before shipment

## Common mistakes

- **Ordering on one great sample** and then receiving a different production batch.
- **Believing "oud" labels** without checking for origin documents.
- **Ignoring your own labelling and fragrance rules.**
- **Mixing up the 6-digit and 8-digit codes** on documents.
- **Not planning for moisture and scent transfer** in shipping.
- **Paying everything before an inspection.**

## FAQ

**Is a licence needed to export agarbatti from India?**
Agarbatti's export policy is listed as free, so it does not need a special export licence in general, but the exporter needs an IEC and standard export documents. Products that contain regulated materials, such as agarwood, need extra permits.

**What is the HSN code for agarbatti?**
3307 41 00 in India's tariff, and 3307 41 in the international HS system.

**Can I buy natural or agarwood incense?**
Yes, but ask for documents showing legal origin and, for agarwood, the export permits under CITES. Prices far below market are a warning.

**Who pays customs duty?**
That depends on the Incoterm you agree and the rules in your country. See our [Incoterms decision tree]({{ '/2026/12/14/incoterms-decision-tree-imports-from-india-which-term-to-choose/' | relative_url }}).

**Does this guide recommend any supplier?**
No. We do not name suppliers. Use the checklist to evaluate any supplier you find.
