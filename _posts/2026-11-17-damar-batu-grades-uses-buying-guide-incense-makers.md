If you make or trade incense, you will hear traders talk about **Damar Batu** in "Bombay quality" and "Calcutta quality". The names sound like places, but they are trade grades. This guide explains what the resin is, what the grades mean, and how to buy it without surprises.

*Grade definitions vary between traders, and this is general information. Always agree specifications in writing and test samples.*

## What is Damar Batu?

Damar Batu is a hard, yellowish-brown, stone-like natural resin collected from certain trees of the dipterocarp family, such as *Shorea* species. Indonesia is by far the largest source of internationally traded Damar Batu, mainly from Sumatra and Kalimantan. Some also comes from other Southeast Asian countries.

Do not confuse it with India's own dammars (such as sal dammar, white dammar, and black dammar), which are different resins from Indian trees. Sal dammar, for example, is widely used in samagri that is burnt in religious ceremonies.

## What is it used for?

* **Incense and dhoop:** it gives a pleasant smell when heated or burnt and is used as a raw material in agarbatti and dhoop manufacturing.
* **Varnish, paints, and putty:** a traditional ingredient in coatings and polishes.
* **Traditional uses:** historically used as a putty for wooden boats.

For incense makers, it is one ingredient among several, so the fragrance recipe and the other binders matter as much as the resin.

## Bombay vs Calcutta grade

Traders commonly describe two main qualities, though exact specs differ by supplier:

| | Bombay quality | Calcutta quality |
|---|---|---|
| Piece size | Large lumps | Smaller or mixed sizes |
| Colour | Yellow to golden brown | Yellow, often with darker pieces mixed in |
| Dust and impurities | Low | Higher |
| Typical position | Higher grade | Lower or mixed grade |

Some traders also sell scraped (extra-cleaned) lots, small "kanni" sizes, and granules or powder. Quality is judged mainly on **piece size, colour, and how much dust comes with the lot**. Ask each supplier for their written definition. "Bombay" from one seller may not match "Bombay" from another.

## Buyer's checklist

1. **Define the grade in numbers.** State minimum piece size, maximum dust or powder percentage, and acceptable colour.
2. **Ask for samples from the actual lot.** Do not rely on a photo or a premium sample.
3. **Inspect for contamination.** Look for bark, soil, stones, and moisture.
4. **Check packing.** Typical bags are around 50 kg up to about 80 kg, often double-layer polypropylene. Confirm bag weight and strength.
5. **Confirm container loading.** The quantity per 20-foot container depends on grade and bag size. Get it in writing.
6. **Agree the price basis.** Compare quotes on the same Incoterm (for example FOB or CIF) and the same grade.
7. **Plan payment carefully.** For a first order, consider staged payment or a letter of credit rather than full advance.
8. **Test before scaling.** Make a small test batch of your incense with the resin before you commit to a large order.

## Red flags

* The supplier refuses a sample or a clear written grade.
* The price is far below every other quote.
* Photos look good but the delivered lot has far more dust or small pieces.
* Pressure to pay the full amount quickly to a personal account.

## Storage tips

Keep the resin in a cool, dry place away from direct heat, since it is combustible and softens with warmth. Use clean, sealed bags to avoid dust and moisture pickup.

## FAQ

**Is Damar Batu the same as gum damar?**
They are related but not identical. Gum damar usually refers to cleaner crystal grades used in paints and varnish, while Damar Batu is the harder, stone-like form used heavily in incense.

**Which grade should a small incense maker buy?**
It depends on your product and price point. Many makers test both and choose the one that gives consistent results at an acceptable cost.

**Can I buy small quantities?**
Often yes from traders, but minimum order quantities vary. Ask each supplier.
