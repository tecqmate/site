## The Ultimate Off-Catalog High-Jewelry Diver

The **Rolex Submariner Date SABR Reference 116659SABR** in 18k white gold is the ultimate luxury evolution of the world's most iconic dive watch. Crafted as an off-catalog piece restricted to VIP clients, its name "SABR" represents its fixed bezel encrusted with **36 baguette blue sapphires and 12 baguette diamonds**, recreating the first 15-minute countdown scale of a traditional dive bezel using gem-set markers. This is complemented by 92 brilliant-cut diamonds pavé-set across the 18k white gold case lugs and crown guards.

Because an authentic factory 116659SABR commands multi-hundred-thousand-dollar valuations on the private secondary market, it is a primary target for aftermarket setters who modify standard 18k white gold Submariners using commercial-grade diamonds and custom gem bezels.

## "SABR-Style" Custom Conversions and Modified Base References

## A 4-Step Secondary Market Buying Protocol

If you are committing six-figure capital to acquire an authentic factory Rolex Submariner SABR, enforce this strict buyer-protection workflow:

* **Step 1: Audit Guarantee Card Reference & Suffix Code:** Verify that the original guarantee card explicitly states `116659SABR`. Never accept verbal claims of "factory set" if the documentation references a plain ceramic bezel model.
* **Step 2: Mandate Independent Gemological Inspection in Escrow:** Send the watch to an accredited vault where a master gemologist inspects the 36 sapphires, 12 bezel diamonds, and 92 case diamonds under 20x magnification to confirm natural origin and factory setting tolerances.
* **Step 3: Inspect 18k White Gold Hallmarks & Glidelock Clasp:** Verify the St. Bernard dog head, 750 gold scales, and Rolex Crown hallmarks stamped under the lugs and inside the Glidelock clasp for deep, razor-sharp outlines without buffed edges.

## Documents to Request

The `SABR` suffix should appear on a serial-matched guarantee card; if the card names `116619LB` or another white gold Submariner, pause and ask for an explanation plus an archival check. Request the box, service records, and any paperwork that describes the gem-set bezel and case pavé. Ask whether the watch has been polished since the stones were set, and whether any of the 92 case diamonds have been replaced. A gem report is useful for describing stone characteristics, but it should be read alongside the watch-specific paperwork rather than in place of it.

- Can the seller show the card, serial, and watch in one photograph?
- Has the bezel or pavé been repaired, and by whom?
- Are the strap, Glidelock clasp, and links original to this watch?
- Will the seller support a direct Rolex reference check?

## Inspection Checklist for the 116659SABR

- Count the 36 blue sapphires and 12 baguette diamonds on the bezel and check that the diamond markers align with the 15-minute scale.

- Inspect the 92 brilliant-cut diamonds across the lugs and crown guards for alignment, clean beds, and consistent clarity.
- Check the 18k white gold hallmarks under the lugs and inside the Glidelock clasp for deep outlines, not buffed edges.
- Operate the Glidelock extension and confirm the clasp closes cleanly with no play.
- Have a watchmaker verify the Calibre 3135 timekeeping against the tolerance stated in the listing and record the result.

## Common Mistakes With SABR Listings

Many buyers treat the "Smurf" association as shorthand for authenticity, when the reference on the card is what actually matters. Others judge the bezel from a single front photograph and miss uneven stone heights at the edges. A third mistake is skipping the clasp: the Glidelock and its hallmarks are part of the watch, not an accessory to be ignored. Finally, do not assume a gem-set diver retains its original water resistance; ask for a current pressure test and have it repeated in escrow if the seller cannot document one.
