In many FMCG distribution businesses, orders still arrive in five different ways: a phone call to the owner, a voice note to a salesman, a photo of a handwritten list, a message in a WhatsApp group and a walk-in at the counter. Each one has to be copied into a bill by someone, and each copy is a chance for a mistake.

The result is familiar. An item is missed, a rate is quoted from memory, stock that has already been sold gets promised again and the retailer calls to ask where the delivery is. This guide shows how to build a simpler order process, step by step, and what to look for in any tool that supports it.

## Quick answer

Give retailers **one place to order**, show them **live stock and current rates** at the moment they order, confirm every order **in writing**, and track it through **picking, dispatch and delivery**. Keep credit limits and outstanding balances visible at the point of ordering. You can start with a simple shared process and move to a platform when volumes grow.

## Where the order process breaks

| Problem | What it causes |
| --- | --- |
| Orders arrive through many channels | Items are missed or entered twice |
| Rates are quoted from memory | Disputes at billing and delivery |
| Retailers cannot see what is in stock | Orders for items that are out of stock, then cancellations |
| No order confirmation | The retailer is unsure whether the order was received |
| No cut-off time for orders | Pressure on picking and delivery, and late dispatches |
| Delivery status is only known by phone | Repeated "where is my order?" calls |
| Outstanding and credit are checked late | Dispatch happens, then payment problems follow |

## A simple order process, step by step

### 1. Set one ordering channel

Choose a single, clearly named way for retailers to place orders. Keep the phone and WhatsApp for questions, not for orders. If you cannot switch overnight, move your biggest or most regular retailers first.

### 2. Keep one product list

Maintain one catalogue with product names, packs, brands and units, and use the same names on bills, in the stock list and in the ordering channel. Duplicate or differently spelled names are a common cause of picking errors.

### 3. Show live stock

Retailers should see whether an item is available before they order. If stock is low, show a limited quantity. This removes the most common reason for cancelled lines.

### 4. Show current rates and schemes

Show the rate the retailer will actually be billed, including any active schemes or discounts, and make clear when a rate was last updated. If a rate changes, the retailer should see the new one, not a remembered old one.

### 5. Make the order simple to repeat

Most retailers reorder similar items every week. A "repeat last order" option, or a saved list, saves time on both sides and reduces mistakes.

### 6. Confirm every order

Send a confirmation with the items, quantities, rates and expected delivery slot. Ask the retailer to check it. A written confirmation is the best defence against later disputes.

### 7. Set order cut-off times

Agree times by which orders must arrive for next-day delivery or for a given route. Cut-offs let your team pick and load in a predictable sequence.

### 8. Check credit and outstanding before dispatch

Make the retailer's balance and credit limit visible at the point of ordering. If an order will exceed the limit, flag it before the goods are loaded, not after.

### 9. Track picking, dispatch and delivery

Give each order a status, such as received, confirmed, being picked, dispatched and delivered. Retailers can then see progress without calling, and your team can see what is stuck.

### 10. Handle returns and shortages cleanly

Record shortages, damaged goods and returns against the order, with a reason. This keeps your stock, your billing and your claims to companies accurate.

## Live stock and rates: why they matter most

Two things cause most of the order problems above: not knowing what is available and not knowing the current price. When both are visible to the retailer at the time of ordering:

- Fewer lines are cancelled or short-supplied
- Billing disputes fall, since the rate was visible from the start
- Your team spends less time answering "is this available?" calls
- Retailers can plan their own stock better

## What to look for in an order platform

If you decide to use software for this, check that it can:

- ✅ Show live stock and current rates to retailers
- ✅ Let retailers place and repeat orders in a few steps
- ✅ Send order confirmations and status updates
- ✅ Keep inventory, orders and delivery in one place
- ✅ Show retailer credit limit and outstanding balance
- ✅ Handle several products, packs and schemes without confusion
- ✅ Work on a phone, since most retailers order from a phone
- ✅ Let you control who sees which prices and products
- ✅ Give you simple reports on what sells and what stalls

## Common mistakes

- **Adding a new channel without retiring the old ones.** You end up with six ways to order instead of five.
- **Showing stock that is already sold.** Live stock needs to match billing.
- **Hiding the rate until billing.** It invites disputes.
- **No order cut-off.** The last-minute rush stays.
- **Ignoring retailers who are not comfortable with apps.** Start with a few willing retailers, and offer help.
- **Measuring nothing.** Track missed lines, cancelled lines and late deliveries before and after you change the process.

## FAQ

**Do I have to move all my retailers at once?**
No. Start with a small group, fix the problems you find and then add more.

**What if a retailer still wants to order by phone?**
Take the order, but enter it into the same system yourself, so there is only one record.

**Is live stock hard to maintain?**
It is only as accurate as your inventory records. Update stock when you receive goods, bill and return items, and do regular stock checks.

**Should credit limits be visible to retailers?**
Many distributors show the available limit or outstanding balance so that retailers can plan. Decide what you want to share, and talk to your accountant about your credit policy.

**How do I know it is working?**
Compare the number of missed lines, cancelled lines, billing disputes and late deliveries before and after.
