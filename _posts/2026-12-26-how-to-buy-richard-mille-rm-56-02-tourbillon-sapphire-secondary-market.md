## The $4M+ Pure Transparency Holy Grail

Limited to an ultra-exclusive production run of just 10 pieces worldwide, the **Richard Mille RM 56-02 Tourbillon Sapphire** represents the absolute extreme of sapphire crystal watchmaking and structural tension architecture. The tripartite case is milled entirely from solid blocks of synthetic sapphire crystal—a process requiring over 1,500 hours of precision CNC machining per watch. 

Suspended inside this completely transparent vessel is a skeletonized grade 5 titanium movement supported by a single braided steel cable measuring just 0.35mm in diameter. Because authentic RM 56-02 examples command multi-million dollar valuations on the private secondary market, acquiring one requires rigorous forensic inspection to detect high-tier acrylic or mineral glass counterfeits, immobile tension pulleys, and unauthorized movement assemblies.

## Acrylic/Glass Shells and Dummy Cable Suspensions
* **What is the problem?** Counterfeiting operations manufacture clear plastic, PMMA acrylic, or low-grade mineral glass case bodies, pairing them with non-functional static wires and PVD-coated alloy movements to simulate the RM 56-02's complex cable architecture.
* **Why does it happen?** True factory sapphire crystal machining destroys dozens of diamond-tipped tools and carries an immense failure rate during production. Replica factories substitute soft acrylic or glass to mimic the visual transparency at a tiny fraction of the cost.

## Due-Diligence Questions for the RM 56-02

- **What exact reference and case number are offered?** Ask the seller to state the reference and show the matching case and movement serials; do not rely on a listing title alone.
- **Can the seller show factory archive or warranty confirmation?** Request documentation tied to that serial, and ask whether Richard Mille or an authorised boutique has confirmed the build.
- **What is known about the cable suspension?** Ask when the tension system was last adjusted and whether the indicator responds normally. Request a video showing the gauge moving as tension changes.
- **Who last serviced the tourbillon?** Ask for invoices and the name of the workshop. A movement of this architecture should not be treated like a routine automatic service.
- **Is the presentation set complete?** Ask for an itemised list of box, warranty, certificate, manual, and straps, with photographs of each.

## Physical Inspection Checklist

- [ ] View the case under neutral light and from several angles; ask the seller to avoid heavily filtered images that hide bubbles or distortion.
- [ ] Have a specialist use non-destructive material testing rather than a home scratch test, which can damage a genuine sapphire case and prove little on its own.
- [ ] Inspect the cable path, pulleys, and tensioners under magnification; ask the specialist to confirm that the parts are functional, not decorative.
- [ ] Observe the tourbillon cage in motion and listen for irregular sounds; confirm the watch keeps time during the inspection period.
- [ ] Check the titanium baseplate and bridge finishing for consistent machining and hand finishing.
- [ ] Verify the case screws, seals, and strap attachments for signs of recent opening by an unauthorised workshop.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Assuming transparency equals sapphire; clear acrylic and mineral glass can look convincing in photography.
- Accepting a static cable or a non-moving indicator as a harmless cosmetic detail.
- Confusing the RM 56-02 with other sapphire Richard Mille references; the architecture and documentation differ.
- Letting an impressive box and certificate substitute for a material test and factory record check.
