When you buy from India, the goods are only half of the deal. The other half is paperwork. Customs in India and in your country, your bank and your insurer all rely on the same set of documents, and a small mismatch between them can hold up a shipment for days.

You do not need to prepare these documents yourself, since the supplier and their customs agent do most of it. But you should know what you are supposed to receive, who issues each document, and what to check before the goods leave India.

## Quick answer

For most shipments, expect a **commercial invoice**, a **packing list** and a **bill of lading** (sea) or **air waybill** (air). Depending on the product and destination, you may also need a **certificate of origin**, quality or plant-health certificates, and an **insurance certificate**. The supplier also files an Indian **shipping bill** for customs. Make sure product descriptions, quantities, values and HS codes **match across every document**.

## The documents, one by one

### 1. Proforma invoice

**Who issues it:** the supplier, before the order is confirmed.
**What it is:** an offer in invoice form, with price, quantity, Incoterm, payment terms and delivery time. It is not a demand for payment by itself.
**Why it matters:** it becomes the reference for everything that follows. Compare each later document against it.

### 2. Commercial invoice

**Who issues it:** the supplier, once the goods are ready to ship.
**What it is:** the main billing document for the shipment, and the basis for customs valuation and duties.
**What to check:**
- Exact buyer and seller names and addresses
- Clear product description, with HS code
- Quantity, unit price, total value and currency
- Incoterm and named place, such as "FCA Mumbai, Incoterms 2020"
- Country of origin
- Payment terms and bank details in the supplier's verified legal name

### 3. Packing list

**Who issues it:** the supplier.
**What it is:** a package-by-package breakdown of what is in the shipment: number of cartons or pallets, contents, weights, dimensions and shipping marks.
**What to check:** total quantity and weights must agree with the commercial invoice. Differences between the two are a common reason customs examines a shipment.

### 4. Shipping bill

**Who files it:** the exporter or their customs agent, through the Indian customs system.
**What it is:** the export declaration filed in India. As a buyer you do not file it, but you can ask for a copy or the shipping bill number once the goods are cleared.
**Why it matters:** it is the official record that the goods left India, and it supports the supplier's own tax and banking paperwork.

### 5. Bill of lading (sea) or air waybill (air)

**Who issues it:** the carrier or shipping line, or the freight forwarder on its behalf.
**What it is:** the contract of carriage and receipt for the goods. A bill of lading can also act as a document of title, which matters if you pay through a bank.
**What to check:**
- Shipper, consignee and notify party are correct
- Description, quantity and weights match the invoice and packing list
- Ports of loading and discharge are right
- The document is "clean", meaning no notes about damaged goods or packaging
- You know whether you receive originals, a telex or seaway release, or a sea waybill, and when

Delays or mistakes here can stop you from collecting the cargo, so check the draft before it is finalised.

### 6. Certificate of origin

**Who issues it:** an authorised body in India, such as a chamber of commerce or an official agency, depending on the type.
**What it is:** proof of where the goods were made.
**When you need it:** some countries ask for it at import, and a preferential certificate may be needed if you want reduced duty under a trade agreement. Ask your customs broker whether you need one and which type.

### 7. Quality, inspection and health certificates

Depending on the product, you may need:
- A quality or inspection certificate
- A plant-health (phytosanitary) certificate for wood, plant or agricultural products
- A fumigation certificate for wooden packaging or certain goods
- Test reports, or compliance documents for regulated products

Check your import rules before ordering, not when the container is already at the port.

### 8. Insurance certificate

**When you need it:** if the supplier arranges insurance, for example under CIF or CIP, or if your bank or contract asks for it.
**What to check:** the amount insured, the risks covered and who is named as beneficiary. See our guide on [Incoterms]({{ '/2026/11/25/incoterms-explained-importing-from-india-exw-fob-cif-ddp/' | relative_url }}) to understand who should arrange it.

### 9. Documents related to payment

If you pay through a bank, such as with a letter of credit or a documentary collection, the bank will check each document against the terms exactly. Even a small mismatch can delay payment or release of the goods. Ask your bank what they need.

## Quick reference table

| Document | Usually issued by | Needed for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proforma invoice | Supplier | Agreeing the deal |
| Commercial invoice | Supplier | Customs value, payment |
| Packing list | Supplier | Customs checks, logistics |
| Shipping bill | Exporter or their customs agent | Indian export clearance |
| Bill of lading / air waybill | Carrier or forwarder | Collecting the cargo |
| Certificate of origin | Authorised body in India | Import rules, duty benefits |
| Quality, health, fumigation certificates | Labs, authorities, specialist firms | Regulated products |
| Insurance certificate | Insurer or broker | Claims and bank requirements |

## What a mismatch looks like

These are the errors that most often cause trouble:

- The invoice says 1,000 units and the packing list says 980
- The product is described differently on the invoice and on the bill of lading
- Different HS codes appear on different documents
- A buyer's name or address contains a typo or an old address
- The Incoterm on the invoice differs from the one in your contract
- The bank account on the invoice is in a different name from the verified supplier

If you spot a mismatch before shipment, it is usually a quick fix. After shipment, it can mean amendment charges and delays.

## Document checklist before the goods ship

- ✅ Proforma invoice agreed and saved as the reference
- ✅ Draft commercial invoice received and checked
- ✅ Draft packing list matches the invoice in quantity and weight
- ✅ HS codes are the same on every document
- ✅ Incoterm and named place match your contract
- ✅ Bank account name matches the verified legal name
- ✅ Draft bill of lading or air waybill reviewed before final issue
- ✅ Certificate of origin arranged, if your broker says you need one
- ✅ Quality, health and fumigation certificates arranged, if required
- ✅ Insurance arranged and the certificate received
- ✅ Copies sent to your customs broker before the goods arrive

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting until the goods arrive** to review the documents.
- **Not sharing the document list with your customs broker** early.
- **Accepting handwritten or edited copies** without a re-issued version.
- **Assuming a certificate is the same as the rule.** A certificate from India does not mean the goods are allowed in your country.
- **Ignoring product-specific rules.** Food, cosmetics, electronics, wood and chemicals each have their own requirements.

## FAQ

**Which documents are mandatory for exporting from India?**
It depends on the product and route. The core set is usually the commercial invoice, packing list, shipping bill and a bill of lading or air waybill. Certificates are added when the product or destination requires them.

**Do I need a certificate of origin?**
Not always. Ask your customs broker. It may be required by your country, or useful if you want to claim reduced duty under a trade agreement.

**Who should handle the shipping bill?**
The Indian exporter or their customs agent. As a buyer, ask for the number or a copy once cleared.

**What if I find an error after the goods have shipped?**
Contact the supplier and your forwarder straight away. Corrections after shipment can cost money and time, so check drafts early.
