## Aftermarket Opal Dial Conversions and Synthetic Composite Slices
* **What is the problem?** Fraudulent secondary dealers take standard 18k white gold Daytona models (like Ref. 126509 or 126519LN), replace the factory dial with an aftermarket synthetic opal slice or resin composite, and market the assembly as an authentic off-catalog factory creation.
* **Why does it happen?** The price delta between a standard white gold Daytona and an authentic factory 1-of-1 stone dial reference exceeds seven figures, tempting dishonest brokers into third-party dial swaps.

## Due-Diligence Questions for the Winter Piece Unique

- **Does the card cite the exact reference?** Ask for a legible image of the `126599 TML` designation and any off-catalogue notation; a standard white gold Daytona code is not enough.
- **What do the stone documents say?** Request any gemological note on the opal and ask whether the slice is natural and un-backed.
- **What service work is recorded?** Ask for invoices and whether the chronograph or case has been opened since delivery.
- **Are serials consistent?** Request close-ups of case, movement, and clasp markings and compare them with the paperwork.
- **Can the seller explain the ownership chain?** For a one-of-one, gaps in history deserve specific answers.

**Can I rely on the card without a physical check?** No. Pair documentary verification with mineralogy and movement inspection.

**Should I price it like a catalogue Daytona?** No. A piece unique is typically valued on its own documentation, condition, and buyer interest.
