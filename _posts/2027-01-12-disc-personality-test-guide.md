## What is the DISC Assessment?

Rooted in the psychological theories of Dr. William Moulton Marston (introduced in his 1928 book *Emotions of Normal People*), the DISC model categorizes human behavior into four primary traits:

1. **D – Dominance:** Direct, results-oriented, firm, strong-willed, and forceful. People high in 'D' focus on bottom lines and conquering challenges.
2. **I – Influence:** Outgoing, enthusiastic, optimistic, high-spirited, and lively. People high in 'I' focus on shaping the environment through persuasion and networking.
3. **S – Steadiness:** Even-tempered, accommodating, patient, humble, and tactful. People high in 'S' focus on cooperation, dependability, and maintaining stability.
4. **C – Conscientiousness:** Analytical, reserved, precise, systematic, and cautious. People high in 'C' focus on quality, accuracy, expertise, and competence.

Most individuals possess a blend of all four styles, but typically one or two traits stand out as primary behavioral drivers.
