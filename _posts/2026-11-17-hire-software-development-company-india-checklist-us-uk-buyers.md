Many US and UK companies hire developers in India to build websites, apps, and software. Done well, it can give you strong engineering at a sensible cost. Done badly, it leads to missed deadlines, unclear ownership of the code, and data-protection problems. This checklist covers what to settle **before** you sign.

## Step 1: Write down what you need

Vague briefs produce vague quotes. Before you contact anyone, prepare:

* **The goal:** what the product must achieve for your customers or business.
* **Must-have features** versus nice-to-have features.
* **Platforms:** web, iOS, Android, or all.
* **Integrations:** payment gateways, CRMs, existing systems.
* **Timeline and budget range:** even a rough range helps you get comparable proposals.

## Step 2: Choose the engagement model

| Model | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed price | Small, well-defined projects | Changes become expensive change requests |
| Time and materials | Projects that will evolve | You need good tracking and regular reviews |
| Dedicated team | Ongoing product development | Needs management time on your side |

## Step 3: Shortlist and check the company

Compare three to five companies on the same brief. Check:

* **Portfolio and case studies** in work similar to yours, and ask to speak to a past client.
* **Team:** who will actually work on your project, and their experience.
* **Company details:** registered business, verifiable address, and how long they have operated.
* **Process:** how they plan sprints, test, and report progress.
* **Communication:** English proficiency, response time, and how many hours overlap with your working day.

## Step 4: Ask for a paid discovery or small pilot

A short paid pilot (a prototype, a small feature, or a technical plan) shows you how the company works before you commit to a large contract.

## Step 5: Settle the contract points

Make sure the contract clearly covers:

* **Ownership of the code and IP.** Your company should own the work you pay for, with written assignment of rights.
* **Access to repositories and servers.** You should hold the admin accounts for your source code, cloud, and domain, not the vendor.
* **Scope, milestones, and acceptance criteria.** Define what "done" means for each stage.
* **Payment schedule tied to milestones.** Avoid paying everything upfront.
* **Change requests.** How changes are priced and approved.
* **Confidentiality (NDA)** and what happens to your data when the project ends.
* **Support and warranty.** How long bugs are fixed free after delivery.
* **Termination rights,** including a handover of everything if the relationship ends.

## Step 6: Handle data protection properly

If the developers will access **personal data** (customer lists, production databases, logs), data-protection law applies.

* **UK businesses:** India does not currently have UK adequacy regulations, so sending or giving access to UK personal data usually needs an approved transfer mechanism, such as the ICO's International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA) or the UK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses. You also need a written data processing agreement with the Article 28 UK GDPR clauses. Remote access from India can count as a transfer.
* **US businesses:** there is no single federal privacy law, but state laws such as California's CCPA and sector rules may apply. Your contract should limit who can see customer data and require breach notification.
* **Best practice for both:** give developers test or anonymised data wherever possible, and use role-based access so people only see what they need.

## Step 7: Check security practices

Ask about:

* Secure coding and code review habits.
* Use of separate environments for development, testing, and production.
* Access control, device security, and use of a VPN.
* Certifications such as ISO 27001 or SOC 2, if relevant to your industry.
* Backup and incident-response plans.

## Red flags

* A very low quote with no clear scope.
* The company cannot show real, verifiable past work.
* Vendor holds the only copy of your source code or admin accounts.
* No written contract, or reluctance to assign IP.
* Pressure to pay the full amount before any work.
* Vague answers about who will work on your project.
* No plan for testing and handover.

## Quick checklist

* Clear written brief with goals and priorities.
* Engagement model chosen to match the project.
* Three to five companies compared on the same brief.
* Past clients contacted.
* Paid pilot or discovery completed.
* Contract covers IP, access, milestones, and termination.
* Data-protection documents in place before developers see personal data.
* Security practices reviewed.

## FAQ

**Is it safe to hire a company in India?**
Many companies deliver excellent work. Safety comes from due diligence, a solid contract, and controlling access to your code and data.

**What if the time zones do not overlap much?**
Agree on a daily overlap window, written updates, and recorded demos so progress stays visible.

**Should I use a freelancer or a company?**
Freelancers can suit small tasks. Companies offer team depth and continuity for larger or ongoing work.
