## The Night Sky of Geneva on Your Wrist

The **Patek Philippe Celestial Reference 6102P-001** in solid platinum is one of the most poetically complex timepieces ever developed by the legendary Geneva manufacture. Featuring a deep blue dial composed of three sapphire crystal discs sliding above one another, the 6102P displays an accurate sky chart of the Northern Hemisphere, tracking the nocturnal sky, the phases and orbit of the moon, and the meridian transit times of Sirius and the moon.

Housed in a substantial 44mm platinum case decorated with hand-engraved Calatrava cross motifs along the case flanks, the 6102P is powered by the ultra-thin automatic Calibre 240 LU CL C featuring an off-center 22k gold micro-rotor. Commanding mid-six-figure to seven-figure valuations on the private secondary market, acquiring a Ref. 6102P requires strict forensic auditing to guard against counterfeit printed dials, damaged sapphire discs, and missing archive documentation.

## Printed Fake Discs, Misaligned Chart Discs, and Missing Provenance
* **What is the problem?** Private secondary-market buyers encounter counterfeit models utilizing static printed plastic dials instead of multi-layered metallized sapphire crystal discs, as well as genuine examples with jammed moon phase gearing or altered case back serial numbers.
* **Why does it happen?** The multi-layered sapphire disc system requires microscopic precision and specialized optical coatings to function. Replica factories substitute printed film or acrylic to simulate the celestial chart at a tiny fraction of the cost.

## Celestial-Specific Due-Diligence Questions

- **Does the Certificate of Origin match the case serial?** Ask for a legible copy and confirm the reference code and configuration through an independent channel.
- **Do all three sapphire discs move correctly?** Request a video showing the sky chart, moon phase, and related indications changing as the crowns are operated gently.
- **Has the watch been serviced by a high-complication specialist?** Ask for invoices and the name of the workshop; astronomical gear trains should not be treated as routine.
- **Are the platinum case and diamond documented?** Ask for hallmark photographs and any paperwork describing the stone at 6 o'clock.
- **Is the presentation set complete?** Request an inventory of the ebony box, winding box, stylus, certificate, straps, and clasp.

## Hands-On Inspection Checklist

- [ ] View the sky chart under magnification and ask a specialist to look for depth, coating damage, or scratched layers.
- [ ] Test each corrector slowly and stop if resistance is felt; never force a high-complication crown during a viewing.
- [ ] Confirm the moon-phase and Sirius indications move in a way consistent with the seller's demonstration and the manual.
- [ ] Inspect Calatrava cross engravings and platinum hallmarks for crisp, hand-finished detail.
- [ ] Have an independent watchmaker check the calibre, micro-rotor, and movement serial through the caseback where appropriate.
- [ ] Photograph the certificate, case numbering, and full set before funds are released.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Accepting a printed star pattern as a substitute for layered sapphire discs.
- Forcing a stuck corrector and turning a minor service issue into a costly repair.
- Treating the winding box or elaborate packaging as proof that the watch is complete and correct.
- Ignoring service history on a watch with multiple astronomical indications.
