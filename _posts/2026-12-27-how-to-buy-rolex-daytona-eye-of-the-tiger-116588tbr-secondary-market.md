## Wild High-Jewelry Glamour Meets Chronograph Power

Unveiled as an off-catalog piece unique style creation, the **Rolex Oyster Perpetual Cosmograph Daytona "Eye of the Tiger" Reference 116588TBR** in 18k yellow gold is one of the most polarizing and exotic high-jewelry timepieces ever created by Rolex. Featuring a fixed bezel encrusted with 36 factory baguette-cut diamonds, the centerpiece is its mesmerizing **tiger-stripe dial**—a pavé setting of brilliant-cut diamonds interwoven with black lacquer waves reminiscent of tiger fur.

Because this wild gem-set reference commands multi-hundred-thousand-dollar valuations on the secondary market, it is heavily targeted by aftermarket diamond setters who modify standard 18k yellow gold Daytona models using low-quality diamonds and printed dials.

---

## Aftermarket "Tiger" Dial Swaps and Custom Bezel Modifications
* **What is the problem?** Dishonest grey-market dealers take standard yellow gold Daytona references (like Ref. 116508 or 116518), drill the bezel, install aftermarket diamond baguettes, and drop in third-party tiger-print dials.
* **Why does it happen?** An authentic factory 116588TBR commands a massive premium over standard yellow gold Daytonas. Third-party conversions cost a fraction of the price to build, allowing bad actors to pocket enormous profit margins by selling modified pieces as genuine factory stock.

## A 4-Step Secondary Market Buying Protocol

If you are committing six-figure capital to acquire an authentic factory "Eye of the Tiger" Daytona, enforce this strict buyer-protection workflow:

* **Step 1: Perform Official Guarantee Card & Suffix Audit:** Ensure the serial-matched guarantee card explicitly displays `116588TBR`. Never accept verbal claims of "factory set" if the documentation references a plain gold model.
* **Step 2: Mandate Independent Gemological & Horological Escrow Inspection:** Ship the watch to an accredited vault where a master gemologist inspects the 36 baguette diamonds and dial pavé setting under 20x magnification to confirm factory setting tolerances.

## Document Pack to Request

Because this is described as an off-catalog creation, the reference on the card matters more than the marketing name. Ask the seller to show a serial-matched guarantee card citing `116588TBR` and to photograph it next to the watch. If the card cites `116508`, `116518`, or another plain yellow gold Daytona, request a written explanation and an archival check before proceeding. Ask for the original box, service history, and any correspondence that refers to the tiger-stripe dial by reference rather than by nickname. A gem report on loose diamonds is useful context, but it cannot prove the bezel setting or dial work is factory original.

- Can the seller show the card and case serial in one legible image?
- Has the dial ever been replaced, refinished, or cleaned by a third party?
- Are the 36 baguette diamonds and the dial pavé the stones supplied with the watch?
- Will an authorized Rolex source confirm the reference against the serial?

## Dial and Setting Inspection Checklist

- Examine the tiger stripes under magnification: raised black lacquer waves should sit within a dense diamond pavé, not appear as flat print on foil.
- Count the 8 baguette diamond hour markers and check that they sit evenly within the pavé field.
- Inspect all 36 bezel baguettes for consistent height, colour, clarity, and channel alignment, especially near the corners.
- Check the Oysterflex strap, its end pieces, and the clasp; ask the seller to show that they belong with this watch.
- Verify the St. Bernard dog head, 750 scales, and crown hallmarks under the lugs without buffed or softened edges.
- Operate the chronograph pushers and reset, then confirm the movement keeps time within the stated chronometer tolerance.

## Common Mistakes With Tiger-Dial Daytonas

The most common trap is buying the look rather than the reference. Aftermarket tiger-print dials and swapped diamond bezels can imitate the silhouette from a distance, so close inspection is essential. Buyers also assume an Oysterflex strap automatically confirms the reference; ask to see the strap and clasp documentation instead. Another mistake is ignoring polishing: aggressive refinishing around a gem-set case can soften hallmarks and disturb settings. Finally, do not accept social media posts or celebrity associations as provenance — ask for factory documentation and an independent verification.

## FAQ

**Is the 116588TBR a regular catalog reference?** It is described as off-catalog; ask the seller for archive confirmation rather than relying on the nickname.

**Can a dial refinish be reversed?** Sometimes, but have a specialist assess the dial before any work; refinishing can reduce collectability.

**Why inspect the pavé as well as the bezel?** Because a correct bezel can sit alongside a replaced or aftermarket dial, and each part needs its own check.
