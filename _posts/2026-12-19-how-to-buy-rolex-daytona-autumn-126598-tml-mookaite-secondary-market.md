Because off-catalog piece unique Rolexes command multi-million dollar valuations on the private secondary market, acquiring one requires extreme due diligence to prevent exposure to aftermarket stone dial swaps, re-cut synthetic jasper slices, or modified standard yellow gold Daytona base references.

## Aftermarket Mineral Dial Swaps and Synthetic Replicas
* **What is the problem?** Counterfeiting operations and third-party workshops remove dials from standard yellow gold Daytona references (like the 126508 or 126518LN) and fit non-factory cut Mookaite jasper slices or printed synthetic glass dials.
* **Why does it happen?** The price delta between a standard yellow gold Daytona and a 1-of-1 factory natural stone dial reference exceeds seven figures, tempting dishonest dealers to market custom modifications as factory originals.

## Due-Diligence Questions for a Piece Unique

- **What exactly does the guarantee card say?** Ask for a legible photograph of the reference field and any off-catalogue designation; do not accept a standard Daytona code as sufficient.
- **Can the seller document the stone's origin?** Request any gemological report or specialist note describing the Mookaite dial, and ask who examined it.
- **What is the service history?** Ask for invoices and whether the movement has been opened since the watch was delivered.
- **Are the case and movement serials consistent?** Request close-ups of the hallmarks and serial engravings, and confirm they appear on the paperwork.
- **Why is this piece being sold?** A clear explanation and ownership timeline are useful when assessing a one-of-one asset.

## Common Mistakes and FAQ

**Can a standard Daytona be converted convincingly?** Custom stone dials exist, which is why documentation and gemology must agree with the case reference.

**Does a natural stone guarantee factory origin?** No. Mineral authenticity and factory build are separate questions; both need evidence.

**Should I assume easy resale because it is one of one?** No. Piece-unique liquidity is often slow and dependent on finding the right buyer.
