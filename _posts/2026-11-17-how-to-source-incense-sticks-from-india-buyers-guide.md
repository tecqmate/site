India is one of the world's biggest sources of incense sticks (agarbatti). Industry reports put Indian incense exports at roughly ₹1,000 crore to ₹1,500 crore a year, with the USA, UAE, and UK among the top destinations. If you plan to import incense for retail, private label, or wellness products, this guide covers how to choose a product, evaluate suppliers, and avoid common mistakes.

*Figures are industry estimates and vary by source. Rules and rates change, so confirm details with your customs broker. This is general information, not legal or trade advice.*

## Step 1: Decide exactly what you want

Suppliers will quote very different prices for products that look similar. Be specific before you ask:

* **Type:** bamboo-stick agarbatti, masala or charcoal-free sticks, charcoal-based sticks, bambooless dhoop sticks, or cones.
* **Fragrance:** natural essential-oil blends, synthetic fragrance, or a mix. Name the scents or share a reference sample.
* **Pack format:** sticks per pack, packs per carton, and whether you want your own brand on the box (private label).
* **Quantity and frequency:** a one-time trial order or a regular monthly or quarterly supply.

A clear spec gets you comparable quotes and shows suppliers that you are a serious buyer.

## Step 2: Know the HS code and duties

Incense sticks are generally classified under **HS 33074100** ("Agarbatti and other odoriferous preparations which operate by burning"). Use this code, plus your destination country's 8 to 10 digit version, when you ask about duties and import rules. Duty rates depend on your country and trade agreements, so check your own tariff schedule or ask your customs broker. Trade-data sites show India exporting around $150 million a year under this code, though figures differ between sources.

## Step 3: Find and shortlist suppliers

Large manufacturing clusters exist in states such as Karnataka and Madhya Pradesh, and many exporters sell through trade directories and trade shows. Shortlist three to five suppliers and compare them on the same spec. Look for:

* A valid **Importer-Exporter Code (IEC)** issued by India's DGFT. Every Indian exporter needs one.
* A registered business with GST registration and a verifiable address.
* Export experience to your region, and references you can check.
* Clear answers about fragrance ingredients and what is in the product.

## Step 4: Ask for samples and test them properly

Never place a large first order without samples.

1. Request samples of the exact product, not a "similar" one.
2. Test several sticks from **different packs**, not just one.
3. Check burn time, how evenly it burns, smoke level, scent strength, ash, and stick quality (straightness, no splinters).
4. Smell the product in an enclosed room as well as open air.
5. Keep a retained sample from each approved batch so you can compare later shipments against it.

## Step 5: Check packaging and labeling

Packaging rules differ from country to country, and it is a common challenge for exporters. Agree in writing:

* Carton size, weight, and how packs are protected from moisture and crushing.
* Language, ingredient and safety text, barcodes, and any marks your market requires.
* Batch numbers or dates, so you can trace problems.

For the EU, UK, and similar markets, ask whether the product meets applicable chemical and labeling rules (for example REACH and CLP in the EU) and request supporting documents.

## Step 6: Agree on price, shipping terms, and payment

* **Price basis:** compare quotes on the same Incoterm, such as FOB (you arrange freight), CIF (supplier includes freight and insurance), or EXW (you collect at the factory).
* **Minimum order quantity (MOQ):** confirm per fragrance and per pack size.
* **Lead time:** from approval of the sample to shipment.
* **Payment:** for a first order, consider a letter of credit or milestone payments. Be wary of anyone who demands 100% advance to an unknown account.
* **Documents:** commercial invoice, packing list, shipping bill, bill of lading or airway bill, and a certificate of origin if you claim preferential duty.

## Red flags

* The supplier will not send samples or refuses a video call.
* The quoted price is far below every other quote.
* No IEC, GST, or verifiable business address.
* Vague answers about ingredients or batch consistency.
* Pressure to pay the full amount quickly to a personal account.
* Samples look great but the bulk shipment differs, so always compare with your retained sample.

## Quick sourcing checklist

* Write a clear product spec and quantity.
* Confirm the HS code and your duties with your customs broker.
* Shortlist three to five suppliers and verify IEC and GST.
* Test samples from multiple packs.
* Agree on packaging, labeling, and batch tracing in writing.
* Compare quotes on the same Incoterm and payment terms.
* Start with a trial order before a large contract.

## FAQ

**What is the usual minimum order?**
It varies by supplier, fragrance, and pack type. Ask for MOQ per item and whether private labeling changes it.

**Can I get my own brand on the packaging?**
Many exporters offer private label. Confirm design approval, artwork costs, and the MOQ for custom packaging.

**How do I avoid sending money to the wrong party?**
Verify the supplier's business details, use traceable payment methods, and prefer staged payments for a first order.
