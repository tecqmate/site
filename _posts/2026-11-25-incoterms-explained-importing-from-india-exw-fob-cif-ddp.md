Almost every quote from an Indian supplier carries three letters: EXW, FOB, CIF or similar. These are **Incoterms**, standard trade terms that say who arranges transport, who pays which costs, and the point where responsibility for the goods passes from seller to buyer.

Choosing the wrong one is an expensive mistake. Two quotes can look equal on price and differ by hundreds or thousands of dollars once freight, export clearance and insurance are counted. This guide explains the rules you are most likely to meet when buying from India.

## Quick answer

For a first order, ask for **FCA** (or **FOB** if shipping by sea in non-containerised form) so the supplier handles export clearance, and choose your own freight forwarder and insurance. Avoid **EXW** unless you have a forwarder in India who can clear exports. Treat **CIF** and **DDP** with care: they look simple, but you give up control of freight and costs.

Always compare quotes on the same term and the same named place.

## What Incoterms do, and do not do

Incoterms are published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC). The current edition is **Incoterms 2020**. The ICC has started preparing a future revision, but no newer edition has been published, so any quote that mentions "Incoterms 2025" or "2026" refers to something that does not exist.

An Incoterm tells you:

- Who arranges and pays for transport
- Who handles export and import customs clearance
- Who buys insurance
- Where risk of loss or damage passes from seller to buyer

An Incoterm does **not**:

- Set the price of the goods
- Decide the payment terms
- Transfer ownership (title)
- Replace a proper sales contract

Write the term, the place and the edition together, for example: "FCA Mumbai (named place), Incoterms 2020".

## The rules you will meet most often

### EXW: Ex Works

The seller makes the goods available at their own premises. You arrange everything else, including loading the truck and export clearance.

- **Good for:** buyers with an experienced forwarder in India.
- **Watch out for:** export clearance. Many buyers assume the seller does it, but under EXW it is the buyer's job, which is hard to do from abroad.
- **Risk:** passes to you at the seller's door.

### FCA: Free Carrier

The seller delivers the goods, cleared for export, to a carrier or place you name, such as your forwarder's warehouse or a container yard.

- **Good for:** most first-time buyers, and for container shipments by sea, air or road.
- **Why it works well:** the seller handles Indian export clearance, while you control freight.
- **Risk:** passes to you when the goods are handed over at the named place.

### FOB: Free on Board

The seller delivers the goods on board the vessel you name at the named port of shipment, cleared for export.

- **Good for:** bulk or non-containerised sea cargo.
- **Watch out for:** for containers, the goods usually reach the terminal before they are loaded on the ship, so FCA often fits better. FOB is for sea and inland waterway only.
- **Risk:** passes to you once the goods are on board.

### CFR: Cost and Freight

The seller pays freight to the destination port, but risk still passes to you at the origin port once goods are on board. You arrange insurance.

### CIF: Cost, Insurance and Freight

Like CFR, but the seller also buys insurance. For CIF, the minimum cover is limited, and the benefit goes to you as the buyer.

- **Good for:** buyers who want a delivered-port price and do not mind the seller choosing the shipping line.
- **Watch out for:** the seller picks the carrier and insurance, and your cover may be thin. You may need extra insurance.
- **Sea only.** For other transport modes, CIP is the equivalent.

### CIP: Carriage and Insurance Paid To

Seller pays carriage and insurance to a named destination, with higher minimum insurance cover than CIF under Incoterms 2020. It works for any transport mode.

### DAP: Delivered at Place

The seller delivers to a named place in your country, ready for unloading, and pays the transport. You handle import clearance and duties.

### DDP: Delivered Duty Paid

The seller delivers to your door with import clearance and duties paid.

- **Good for:** small shipments where you want everything handled.
- **Watch out for:** it requires the seller to clear imports in your country, which many Indian suppliers cannot or will not do properly. Prices are often higher, and costs are less visible.

## Which term for which situation?

| Your situation | Often suitable | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First order, container, you have a forwarder | FCA | Seller clears export, you control freight |
| Bulk cargo by sea | FOB | Classic term for port-to-port sea cargo |
| You want a single price to your port | CIF or CFR | Easy to budget, but less control |
| Any transport mode, insurance included | CIP | Better cover than CIF |
| Small parcel, you want it handled | DAP or DDP | Convenient, check what is included |
| You have a trusted forwarder in India | EXW | Maximum control, more work |

This table is a starting point, not a rule. Your product, route and volume matter.

## How to compare quotes properly

Suppose two suppliers quote for the same goods:

- Supplier 1: EXW, lower price
- Supplier 2: FOB, higher price

The EXW quote may look cheaper, but you still need to add export clearance, inland transport, loading and port charges, which the FOB price already includes. Convert both to the same term before comparing, or ask each supplier to quote the same Incoterm and named place.

Also check:

- **The named place.** "FOB Mumbai" and "FOB Chennai" are different offers.
- **Who pays port handling charges** at origin and destination.
- **The validity of the quote.** Freight and currency change.
- **What is not included.** Import duties, taxes, brokerage and inland delivery at your end are usually separate unless the term is DDP.

## Insurance: do not skip it

Under many terms, risk passes to you before the shipment reaches your warehouse. If goods are damaged or lost in transit and you are not insured, you may bear the loss. Even under CIF, check what cover is included, because the minimum can be limited. Ask your broker or insurer what is suitable for your goods.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the term without the place.** "FOB" alone is incomplete.
- **Choosing FOB or CIF for container cargo** when FCA or CIP may fit the real handover better.
- **Assuming EXW includes export clearance.** It does not.
- **Mixing up the term and the payment.** Incoterms say nothing about when or how you pay.
- **Accepting DDP without asking who pays which taxes** and who is the importer of record.
- **Relying on the supplier's freight forwarder** without checking costs and routes.

## A short checklist before you accept a quote

- ✅ The quote states the Incoterm, the named place and "Incoterms 2020"
- ✅ You know who clears export and who clears import
- ✅ You know where risk passes to you
- ✅ Insurance is arranged, and the cover is clear
- ✅ Freight, port, documentation and inland charges are itemised
- ✅ Payment terms are written separately in the contract
- ✅ You have compared all quotes on the same term

## FAQ

**Which Incoterm is best for importing from India?**
There is no single best term. FCA (or FOB for non-containerised sea cargo) is a common, balanced choice for first orders, because the seller clears export and you keep control of freight.

**Is FOB the same as CIF?**
No. Under FOB, you pay for the main freight and insurance. Under CIF, the seller arranges and pays for freight and minimum insurance to your port, though risk still passes to you earlier, at the loading port.

**Do Incoterms cover payment or ownership?**
No. They deal with delivery, costs and risk. Payment and title are set in your contract.

**Is DDP safe for a small buyer?**
It can be convenient, but you give up control and visibility of costs. Confirm exactly who is the importer of record and what taxes are included.
