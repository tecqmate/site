## The Original Gem-Set GMT Icon

Introduced as an off-catalog masterpiece to celebrate the 50th anniversary of the GMT-Master line, the **Rolex GMT-Master II SARU Reference 116758SARU** in 18k yellow gold is one of the most recognizable high-jewelry tool watches ever produced. Its name "SARU" derives from its bi-directional rotating bezel set with **18 baguette blue sapphires, 18 baguette rubies, 23 baguette diamonds, and 1 triangular diamond** at 12 o'clock—recreating the iconic "Pepsi" blue and red colorway using precious gemstones.

Because factory SARU models command massive six-figure premiums on the global secondary market, dishonest dealers frequently modify standard 18k yellow gold GMT-Master II models (such as Ref. 116710 or 116718LN) with third-party gem-set bezels and custom pavé lugs.

---

## Custom "SARU-Style" Bezel Conversions and Lug Modifications
* **What is the problem?** Unscrupulous grey-market brokers take standard yellow gold GMT-Master II references, install aftermarket sapphire/ruby/diamond bezels, and drill/set diamonds into the lugs and crown guards to imitate an authentic factory 116758SARU.
* **Why does it happen?** The value difference between a standard yellow gold GMT-Master II and a genuine factory 116758SARU can exceed $100,000 to $200,000, creating strong financial incentives for aftermarket alterations.

## A 4-Step Secondary Market Buying Protocol

If you are committing six-figure capital to acquire a factory Rolex GMT-Master II SARU, enforce this strict buyer-protection workflow:

* **Step 1: Audit Guarantee Card Reference & Suffix Code:** Verify that the original guarantee card explicitly states `116758SARU`. Never accept verbal claims of "factory set" if the papers cite a standard black ceramic model.
* **Step 2: Mandate Independent Gemological Inspection in Escrow:** Send the watch to an accredited vault where a master gemologist inspects the 18 sapphires, 18 rubies, and 24 bezel diamonds under 20x magnification to confirm natural origin and factory channel-setting precision.
* **Step 3: Verify Calibre 3186 GMT Movement Functionality:** Inspect the automatic movement to ensure smooth independent hour hand adjustment (jumping local hour) and check the date change mechanism at midnight.
* **Step 4: Check Gold Hallmarks Under Loupe:** Verify the St. Bernard dog head, 750 gold scales, and Rolex Crown hallmarks stamped beneath the lugs and Oyster clasp to ensure deep, crisp outlines without buffed edges.

## Documents to Ask For

Start with the guarantee card, because the `SARU` suffix is the clearest written link to this reference. Ask for a single legible photograph showing the card reference, serial number, and the watch together. If the card names `116718LN` or another standard gold GMT, request a written explanation and an independent archival check before proceeding. Request the box, service history, and any paperwork describing the gem-set bezel. A gem certificate is helpful for describing stone origin and treatments, but it does not establish that the bezel was fitted at the factory.

- Does the card reference match the case serial in one image?
- Has the bezel or lug setting been repaired, replaced, or re-polished?
- Can the seller identify who last serviced the watch and when?
- Will the seller allow a gemologist to examine the bezel stones in person?

## Inspection Checklist for the 116758SARU

- Count the 18 blue sapphires, 18 rubies, 23 baguette diamonds, and the triangular diamond at 12; compare the count to the listing and paperwork.
- Check the colour boundary between sapphires and rubies for even transitions and consistent stone height under 10x magnification.
- Inspect the diamond lug and crown-guard pavé for symmetry, clean metal beds, and no rough drill edges.
- Rotate the bezel through its clicks and confirm it seats correctly at each position; note any gritty or loose feel.
- Test the independent hour hand on the Calibre 3186 and then verify the date changes correctly at midnight.
- Check the St. Bernard dog head, 750 scales, and crown hallmarks under the lugs and on the Oyster clasp.

## Common Mistakes With SARU Listings

A frequent error is treating the colour pattern as proof of factory work; a skilled setter can reproduce the blue-red effect, so the card and the setting quality both matter. Buyers also miscount the bezel stones, forgetting the triangular diamond at 12, which leads to confusion when comparing listings. Another mistake is skipping the jump-hour test even though it is central to the GMT function. Finally, do not assume ruby colour alone indicates natural origin — ask the gemologist to address treatments explicitly in the report.

## FAQ

**Does the bezel still rotate?** Ask the seller to demonstrate it and have the escrow inspector repeat the check; do not assume a gem-set bezel is fixed.

**Is a missing card recoverable?** Possibly, but without it you need strong alternative documentation and a direct Rolex check; treat the gap seriously.

**Can treated rubies appear in a factory-set watch?** Ask the gemologist to report on treatments and have any wording in the paperwork clarified before relying on it.
