## 1. The Asymmetric Aerodynamic Beast

Designed in collaboration with Olympic sprinter Yohan Blake, the **Richard Mille RM 61-01 Yohan Blake** is renowned for its distinctive **asymmetric case design**. Tapering in thickness and width between 9 o'clock and 3 o'clock, the case architecture is engineered to reduce aerodynamic drag and prevent the crown from rubbing against an athlete's wrist during high-velocity sprints.

Combining a ultra-tough **TZP (Tetragonal Zirconia Polycrystal) black ceramic** front and back bezel with a central case middle crafted from carbon, the RM 61-01 commands high six-figure valuations on the secondary market. However, because of its popularity among modern collectors, the market is rife with high-clone composite cases and modified standard movements.

## 2. Counterfeit Ceramic Finishing and Cloned Skeleton Movements
* **What is the problem?** Counterfeiting workshops utilize cheap black PVD-coated steel or low-grade resin composite to replicate the matte finish of TZP ceramic and the layered striations of carbon.
* **Why does it happen?** The price delta between a replica case assembly and an authentic RMUL2 skeletonized movement with Grade 5 titanium bridges generates massive financial incentive for fraudulent secondary dealers.

## Beyond the Matrix: RM 61-01 Due Diligence

The RM 61-01 is defined by its asymmetry, so the first question is not "is it black?" but "does the case actually taper?" Ask the seller for side-profile photographs taken square to the case at both 9 o'clock and 3 o'clock, and ask them to describe where the case thins. The design was developed with sprinter Yohan Blake to reduce drag and keep the crown away from the wrist; a uniformly deep case suggests a generic shell rather than the intended ergonomic architecture. Have an independent watchmaker verify the profile in person rather than relying on perspective-corrected marketing images.

Material verification is the second track. TZP black ceramic and the carbon case middle behave differently under light and under a loupe. Ask whether either bezel has been replaced, refinished, or coated, and whether the case has ever been opened outside an authorized service network. Request close images of the bezel edge and case-middle junction, where paint or PVD over metal often reveals itself as chipping or a metallic glint. Ask how the green and yellow accents on the upper bridges were applied; hand-painted details should show deliberate, clean lines rather than uniform transfer printing. If the seller cannot answer basic questions about the Jamaican color cue, they may be describing a generic skeleton watch.

Movement and paperwork questions should be explicit. Ask which caliber is present, whether it has been serviced, and whether the free-sprung balance and double barrels function as expected. Ask for the NFC or digital warranty record and how it was validated; a screenshot is not the same as a check with an authorized service point. Ask for the guarantee folder, box, and any delivery documents, and require that serials be photographed together. ### Expanded Inspection Checklist for the Yohan Blake Reference

* **Side profile:** Compare the 9 o'clock and 3 o'clock thickness in person; the taper should be evident without camera angles.
* **Ceramic surface:** Look for a deep matte black finish under magnification, with no metallic sheen or coated chips at edges.
* **Carbon middle:** Examine the layered structure for consistent striations and clean transitions into the ceramic bezels.
* **Bridge accents:** Ask an independent specialist to confirm that green and yellow details are hand-applied with crisp boundaries.
* **Skeleton movement:** Confirm the expected beat rate and that decorative plates are not masking a conventional movement.
* **Screws and caseback:** Inspect spline screw heads for clean edges and check that caseback serials match the folder and card.

### Common Mistakes With RM 61-01 Listings

* Judging asymmetry from a single front photograph where perspective hides the taper.
* Assuming any black case is ceramic without material verification.
* Mistaking printed bridge colors for hand-painted detailing.
* Accepting a digital card image without an independent validation path.
* Ignoring service history on a watch engineered for high-stress sport use.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does every RM 61-01 use the same ceramic-and-carbon construction?** Ask the seller to specify the case materials on the exact watch offered and describe the bezel and case-middle junction. Material confirmation should be item-specific rather than assumed from the model name.

**Can the asymmetry be judged from listing photos?** Only cautiously. Ask for orthogonal side profiles and confirm in person. Perspective and camera angle can make a symmetrical case appear tapered, so independent measurement is preferable.

**Are the green and yellow bridge accents original?** Ask when they were applied and whether any restoration occurred. Have an independent specialist verify hand-applied detailing under magnification rather than relying on color appearance.

**Is an NFC card image enough?** No. Ask how the check was performed and whether an authorized service point confirmed the serial. Treat an unverified image as a claim, not proof.
