Agarbatti making is one of the most accessible small manufacturing businesses in India. You can start small, hire local workers, and grow into packaged retail or even export. The part many beginners skip is **proper training**, which affects fragrance quality, burn quality, and cost. This guide covers where to learn, what support exists, and how to plan your first steps.

*Schemes, courses, and fees change. Always confirm current details with the organisation before you apply or pay. This is general information, not financial advice.*

## Quick answer

Learn the process before you buy a machine, then make small batches and sell them locally until quality and cost are consistent. Choose hand rolling, a pedal machine, or an automatic unit based on the orders you already have—not on the largest capacity you can afford. Keep a written cost per packet that includes raw material, labour, packaging, power, and transport, and check current scheme details before relying on any support.

## Where to get trained: FFDC Kannauj

The **Fragrance & Flavour Development Centre (FFDC)** in Kannauj, Uttar Pradesh is an autonomous body under the Ministry of MSME. It runs training programmes for the fragrance and flavour industry at several levels, including one-week programmes, Level 1 to Level 3 professional courses, and a one-year diploma. It has also offered short, hands-on courses that cover **agarbatti, dhoopbatti, havan samagri, and basic perfumery**.

What a good course should teach you:

* How agarbatti is made, from raw materials to drying and packing.
* How fragrance oils and aroma chemicals are chosen and blended.
* Quality checks such as burn time, scent strength, and consistency.
* Basic safety and regulatory awareness for fragrance products.

Course dates, eligibility, and fees change from year to year, so check FFDC's current schedule rather than relying on old listings.

## Government support for artisans

The MSME ministry has run an **agarbatti industry programme** to support artisans with training, raw material, machines, and marketing. Under the Khadi Gramodyog Vikas Yojana (KGVY), the agarbatti component is aimed at generating employment, especially for women artisans and unemployed youth. According to the scheme guidelines:

* Training is organised through centres of excellence such as FFDC Kannauj and VNIT Nagpur.
* Beneficiaries can receive support such as pedal-operated or automatic agarbatti machines.
* The skill development programme runs for about 10 days, in batches of up to 20 candidates.
* The beneficiary age group in the guidelines is 18 to 55 years.

Check with your local KVIC or district industries office for who is eligible and how to apply in your area.

## Machine options: manual, pedal, or automatic

| Option | Best for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Hand rolling | Very small start, premium hand-rolled products | Slow, output depends on skill |
| Pedal-operated machine | Home or small unit with low investment | Limited output |
| Automatic machine | Higher volumes and consistent sticks | Higher cost, needs power and space |

Start with the option that matches your budget and market, and plan to upgrade once your sales are steady.

## Raw materials to plan for

* **Bamboo sticks** of consistent thickness and straightness.
* **Base powders and binders**, including natural resins such as dammar in some formulations.
* **Fragrance oils** or essential oils.
* **Packaging** such as sleeves, cartons, and labels.

Buy small test quantities first and ask for samples. Quality differences between suppliers show up only after you burn the sticks.

## A simple startup checklist

1. **Get trained.** Take a hands-on course before spending on machines.
2. **Pick your product.** Masala, charcoal-based, natural, or dhoop sticks, and your price band.
3. **Test recipes.** Make small batches and check burn time, smoke, and scent.
4. **Register your business.** Consider Udyam registration, and GST registration if required for your turnover.
5. **Plan costs.** Add raw materials, labour, packaging, power, and transport. Know your cost per packet.
6. **Choose how you will sell.** Local shops, wholesalers, online, or private label.
7. **Think about exports later.** Once you have steady quality, you can learn the export process, including the HS code 33074100.

## Mistakes beginners make

* Buying machines before learning the process.
* Using cheap raw materials that give inconsistent burn.
* Ignoring packaging, which affects shelf life and shelf appeal.
* Not costing every packet, so selling at a loss.
* Skipping small test batches before large orders.

## FAQ

**Can I learn without a formal course?**
Many people start by working in a unit, but a structured course shortens the learning curve and helps you avoid costly mistakes.

**How much money do I need?**
It depends on scale. A hand-rolling or pedal-machine start needs far less than an automatic unit. Prepare a written cost plan before you decide.

**Can women and first-time entrepreneurs get support?**
The agarbatti programme specifically mentions women artisans and unemployed youth. Check current guidelines for eligibility.

**Is a GST registration needed from day one?**
It depends on your turnover, sales channel, and the rules in force. Ask a local accountant and check the current official guidance before you register or skip registration.

**Which product should I make first?**
Choose one you can make consistently and explain clearly to a buyer. A narrow first range makes quality control and costing much easier than a long list of fragrances.

## A simple costing worksheet

Before you scale, fill this in for one packet size:

| Cost head | What to record |
| :--- | :--- |
| Bamboo sticks | Price per bundle, sticks per bundle, wastage allowance |
| Powder and binders | Weight per batch, price per kilogram, rejected batches |
| Fragrance | Quantity per stick or batch and supplier quote |
| Packaging | Sleeve, carton, label, and outer box |
| Labour | Time per batch and agreed wage |
| Power and consumables | Machine running time and small supplies |
| Transport | Cost to reach the first customer or wholesaler |

Divide the total by the number of saleable packets, not the number you hoped to produce. Rework the sheet after every supplier quote change. A recipe that smells right but loses money on paper will not improve with volume.
