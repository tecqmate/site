## Aftermarket Dial Repaints and Modified Base Movements
* **What is the problem?** Counterfeiting operations take standard grey or black dial Offshore references, repaint the *Méga Tapisserie* grid in turquoise with yellow accents, and present them as rare factory colorways.
* **Why does it happen?** The price delta between a standard secondary Offshore and a rare factory vibrant colorway can reach tens of thousands of dollars, incentivizing illicit dealers to perform third-party dial conversions.

## Reference-Specific Baseline for the 26238TI

Before viewing, write down what the listing claims: a 42mm Royal Oak Offshore chronograph in grade 5 titanium, sandblasted and satin-brushed, with a turquoise dial, yellow sub-dial accents, rubber pushers and a matching integrated rubber strap, powered by the Calibre 4404. Each of those details is a checkable question. Titanium should feel noticeably lighter than a steel case of the same size, and the surfaces should show the mixed satin and blasted finishing typical of the reference. The *Méga Tapisserie* pattern and the yellow printing should look crisp under magnification, with no bleeding at the edges of the sub-dials. If the seller cannot describe the colourway precisely, or calls it by a name that does not match the reference, ask for corrected documentation before proceeding.

## Inspection Checklist

- **Case and bevels:** check the transitions between sandblasted and polished surfaces; uneven or rough bevels deserve a second opinion.
- **Dial printing:** examine the tapisserie grid, yellow minute track and sub-dial printing under a loupe; look for paint bleed or uneven saturation.
- **Crystal and gaskets:** check for chips and ask when the case was last pressure-tested; rubber components age even on little-worn watches.
- **Pushers and crown:** operate them gently and note the resistance; the rubber pushers should seat cleanly without sticking.
- **Chronograph function:** have a watchmaker verify column-wheel flyback operation and reset behaviour rather than relying on a short demonstration.
- **Strap mechanism:** test the quick-release system for smooth engagement and absence of play, and confirm the correct spare straps are included.
- **Bracelet alternative:** if a metal bracelet is offered, check its fit and end-link finish; aftermarket parts should be disclosed.

## Questions for the Seller

Ask for the digital warranty record and confirm, through an official channel, that the dial colour and reference match. Request service history, the date of the last gasket-related work, and confirmation of whether the watch has been refinished. Ask for a complete list of accessories and strap options. Make the sale conditional on an in-person inspection by a watchmaker familiar with AP chronographs, and agree on the outcome if the report is inconclusive.

## Common Mistakes

A common error is judging titanium by colour alone, since a painted steel case can look similar in photographs. Another is accepting a repaint as a rare factory dial without comparing it against documented examples. Buyers also overlook the strap system: a sticky or loose quick-release mechanism affects daily use and can be expensive to correct.
