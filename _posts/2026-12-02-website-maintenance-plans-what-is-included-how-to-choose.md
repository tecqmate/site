Many businesses spend weeks on a new website and then forget about it. A year later, the contact form has stopped working, the site is slow, the plugins are out of date and nobody knows who has the login details.

A website is closer to a vehicle than to a brochure. It needs regular servicing to stay safe, fast and working. A maintenance plan is how you pay for that servicing.

## Quick answer

A good maintenance plan covers **software updates, backups, security monitoring, uptime checks, bug fixes and a clear support process**, with defined response times and a written list of what is and is not included. Choose a plan based on how important your site is to your business, not on the lowest monthly price.

## Why maintenance matters

- **Security.** Outdated software and plugins are a common way sites get hacked.
- **Reliability.** Browsers, hosting and third-party services change. Things that worked last year can break.
- **Speed.** Sites slow down over time as content, plugins and traffic grow.
- **Search visibility.** Slow, broken or insecure sites can lose ranking and customers.
- **Cost control.** Fixing small problems early is usually cheaper than emergency repairs.

## What a maintenance plan usually includes

| Area | What it means | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Updates | Updating the core platform, themes, plugins and libraries | Closes security holes and prevents breakage |
| Backups | Regular copies of files and database, stored away from the server | Lets you recover from errors or attacks |
| Security | Malware scans, firewall rules, access reviews, SSL certificate renewal | Protects your data and your customers |
| Monitoring | Uptime alerts, error logs, speed checks | You find out about problems before customers do |
| Bug fixes | Fixing things that stop working | Keeps the site doing its job |
| Support | A way to ask for help, with response times | Gets problems solved quickly |
| Reporting | A short summary of work done and issues found | Shows what you are paying for |

## What is often not included

Read the plan carefully. These items are commonly extra:

- **New features** or redesigns
- **Content updates**, such as writing text or adding products
- **Marketing and SEO work**
- **Hosting fees, domain renewals and paid plugin licences**
- **Fixing problems caused by changes made by someone else**
- **Recovering a site that was already hacked or neglected** before the plan started
- **Out-of-hours or emergency support**

None of these are wrong to exclude, but you should know where the boundary is.

## Types of plans

| Plan type | How it works | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed monthly plan | A set fee for a defined list of tasks | Most small and mid-sized business sites |
| Hours bank | You buy a block of hours to use as needed | Sites with changing needs |
| Pay as you go | You pay for each request | Very small or rarely changed sites |
| Retainer with a dedicated person | Reserved capacity from a developer or team | Business-critical or busy sites |

Pay as you go looks cheapest but offers no safety net. When something breaks at a bad moment, you may wait or pay a premium.

## How to choose a plan: ten questions

1. **What exactly is included?** Ask for a written list of tasks and how often each is done.
2. **How often are backups taken, and where are they stored?** Backups kept only on the same server are not enough.
3. **Can you show me a restore test?** A backup that has never been restored is an unproven backup.
4. **What are the response and fix times?** Ask for response time for urgent issues and for normal requests.
5. **How is support offered?** Email, chat, phone, a ticket system, and in which hours and time zones.
6. **How do you handle updates safely?** A good team tests updates on a staging copy before they go live.
7. **What security measures are included?** Ask about scans, firewalls, login protection and certificate renewal.
8. **Who owns the accounts and access?** Your domain, hosting and code accounts should be in your company's name.
9. **What are the contract terms?** Check minimum period, notice period and what happens to your files when you leave.
10. **What reports do I get?** A short monthly summary is enough, but it should exist.

## Red flags

- A very cheap plan with no written list of what is covered
- No clear answer on backups, or only backups stored on the same server
- The vendor holds the only admin access or owns your domain
- No staging environment for testing updates
- No response-time commitments
- Long lock-in periods with no way to leave
- Reports are missing, or are only automated and unclear

## What you should do yourself

Even with a plan, a few responsibilities stay with you:

- **Keep ownership of your accounts.** Domain, hosting, code repository and analytics should be registered to your business.
- **Store credentials safely.** Use a password manager, and give access to named people only.
- **Tell the team about changes.** A new payment gateway, a marketing tool or a campaign launch can all affect the site.
- **Review the monthly report** and ask about anything unclear.
- **Test important paths now and then.** Place a test order, submit your own contact form and check you receive it.

## Maintenance plan checklist

- ✅ A written list of included tasks and their frequency
- ✅ Regular backups stored away from the main server, with a tested restore
- ✅ Updates tested on a staging copy before release
- ✅ Security scans, login protection and certificate renewal covered
- ✅ Uptime monitoring with alerts
- ✅ Defined response times for urgent and normal issues
- ✅ Clear support channels and hours
- ✅ Domain, hosting and code accounts in your company's name
- ✅ A short monthly report
- ✅ Contract with a reasonable notice period and a handover clause

## Common mistakes

- **Buying on price alone.** The cheapest plan may include very little.
- **No backups outside the server.** If the server fails, the backup fails with it.
- **Not owning your own accounts.** This makes changing vendors difficult.
- **Waiting until something breaks** to look for support.
- **Assuming hosting includes maintenance.** Hosting keeps the server running. It usually does not update your site.

## FAQ

**Do I need a maintenance plan for a small site?**
Most business sites benefit from at least basic updates, backups and security checks. Very small, rarely changed sites might manage with pay-as-you-go support, but it is worth having someone you can call.

**How often should a website be updated?**
Security updates should be applied soon after they are released, after testing. Other updates can follow a regular monthly schedule.

**What is the difference between hosting and maintenance?**
Hosting provides the server where your site lives. Maintenance keeps the website software itself up to date, secure and working.

**Can my original developer do the maintenance?**
Often yes, and it can be efficient since they know the code. Agree the plan and response times in writing, as you would with any vendor.

**What if I already have a neglected site?**
Ask for an audit first. A good provider will check updates, security and backups, and give you a clear list of fixes before they quote for ongoing care.
